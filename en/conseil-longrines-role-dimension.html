Tie beams: what are they for and how are they sized?

Invisible once the ground slab is poured, tie beams are nevertheless a key element of a house's seismic resistance: they stop the foundations from moving independently of one another.

Published 16 November 2026 · 4 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

A tie beam is a reinforced concrete beam that connects the footings to each other at foundation level. It has two roles: tying the foundations together so that they move as one in an earthquake or under settlement, and often carrying the walls of the ground floor. In Morocco, the seismic code RPS 2000 (2011 version) requires pad footings to be tied together.

Why tie beams are essential in seismic areas

During an earthquake, the ground does not move the same way everywhere. Unconnected pad footings can move closer together or further apart, tearing the columns away at their base. The tie beam then works as a tie (in tension) or a strut (in compression) between the supports. It also limits the effects of differential settlement by sharing the loads better between neighbouring foundations.

The two types of tie beam

  • Connecting tie beam: it connects the footings and carries little load. Its cross-section remains modest and its reinforcement is symmetrical (top and bottom), as it may be in tension or compression.
  • Load-bearing tie beam: it receives the weight of a wall or heavy partition and also works in bending. It is designed as a beam resting on the footings.

What cross-section and reinforcement?

The cross-section of a tie beam depends on the span between footings, the wall loads and the seismic force to be transmitted. Seismic codes set a minimum tie force and minimum cross-sections; these values vary between texts (Moroccan RPS, Algerian RPA, Eurocode 8) and must not be transposed from one country to another. On a villa, typical tie beams have cross-sections of a few tens of centimetres per side, with regular stirrups, but the exact cross-section is to be determined by the engineer from the design calculations.

Tie beams and ground slab

The ground-floor slab does not replace the tie beams, unless the engineer has designed a suspended slab or a raft that plays this role. A slab laid on hardcore is an independent element, which may crack without compromising the structure. Tie beams also support the façade walls and limit rising damp when a damp-proof course is provided above them (see rising damp).

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm draws the foundation plan with all the tie beams, their cross-sections and their reinforcement, in accordance with RPS 2011. During site visits, it checks that the tie beams have not been omitted or reduced to save money, a practice still common on small sites and with serious consequences in an earthquake.

Frequently asked questions

Are tie beams mandatory in Morocco?

The seismic code RPS 2000 (2011 version) requires pad footings to be tied together; tie beams are the usual way of doing so.

What is the difference between a tie beam and a strip footing?

A strip footing transmits the load of a wall directly to the soil, whereas a tie beam is a beam that connects footings and can carry a wall between two supports.

What cross-section for a tie beam?

It depends on the span, the walls carried and the seismic force: the cross-section and reinforcement are set by the engineer in the foundation drawings.
