Land title and a house's compliance: what you need to check

A land title in order says nothing about what has been built on it. To buy with peace of mind, you must check ownership and the compliance of the construction separately.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

The land title establishes who owns the land and what rights encumber it (mortgages, easements, objections). The compliance of the construction depends on other documents: the building permit, the approved plans and the occupancy permit or certificate of compliance. A buyer must check both, as one does not guarantee the other.

Checking ownership: the land title

  • Certificate of ownership: request it from the land registry (ANCFCC) with the title number, and have it dated shortly before signing. It shows the registered owner, the description of the property, charges and entries.
  • Mortgages: if the seller has a loan, the release must actually be registered; a loan paid off without a release leaves the mortgage on the title.
  • Objections and seizures: these can block the sale.
  • Co-ownership: for a flat, check that the title is properly individualised and that co-ownership regulations exist.
  • Description: the title describes the property (bare land, house, number of storeys). An update may be needed if the construction has changed.

Checking the construction: the authorisations

Building permitThe project was authorisedSeller, municipality, Rokhas platform
Approved plansWhat could be built (storeys, areas, setbacks)Seller, architect, municipality
Occupancy permit or certificate of complianceThe completed construction was certified compliantSeller, municipality

The occupancy permit is provided for by Law 12-90 on urban planning. It is issued after completion of the works, once compliance with the permit has been certified. Its absence does not always prevent the sale, but it must be explained: see our article on houses without a permit or non-compliant.

The essential check: comparing plans and reality

The documents may be in order and the house different: a storey added after the occupancy permit, an enclosed terrace, an undeclared pool. Compare the approved plans with what exists, room by room and level by level. If there are discrepancies, question the seller, the notary and the municipality before signing.

Who does what?

  • The notary checks the title, the seller's identity and the entries; they do not inspect the physical condition of the building.
  • The municipality and the urban agency provide information on authorisations and any offences; they are also where you check that no offence report has been drawn up.
  • The engineering firm compares the building with the plans, checks the structure and services, and reports discrepancies.

Frequently asked questions

Does the land title prove that the house is compliant?

No. It proves ownership and lists rights and charges; the compliance of the construction is checked with the building permit, the approved plans and the occupancy permit.

How can you check that a land title is free of mortgages?

By requesting a recent certificate of ownership from the land registry (ANCFCC): it lists the registered mortgages, seizures and objections.

Does the notary check the condition of the house?

No. The notary checks the title, the entries and the deed; the technical condition and compliance with the plans are a matter for an architect or an engineering firm.
