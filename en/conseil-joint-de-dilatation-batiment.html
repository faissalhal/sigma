Building expansion joints: when are they needed and how often?

A concrete building expands in the heat and contracts in the cold. Beyond a certain length, it must be split into independent blocks to avoid cracking.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

An expansion joint splits the building into independent blocks to absorb changes in length due to temperature and concrete shrinkage. The French BAEL rules, often used in Morocco, recommend a joint about every 25 m in dry, hot regions with large temperature swings (close to the Mediterranean climate), and up to 50 m in humid, temperate regions. In Marrakech, a block length of around 25 m is therefore most often adopted, unless justified by calculation.

Why joints are necessary

Concrete expands by about 1 mm per 10 m of length for a 10 °C difference. Between a winter night and a sunny summer day, a roof terrace in Marrakech can undergo several tens of degrees of variation. If it cannot move freely, a long building accumulates forces that crack the walls, slabs and parapets. Concrete shrinkage on drying has the same effect.

Orders of magnitude

StructureDistance between joints (indicative)
Reinforced concrete structure, hot and dry climateAbout 25 m (BAEL rules, Mediterranean regions)
Reinforced concrete structure, humid and temperate climateUp to 40 to 50 m
Masonry façade wallsLower values, around 20 m in a Mediterranean climate
Ground slabs, terraces, parapetsCloser jointing, depending on the finish

These distances are benchmarks: the engineer may increase them by calculating the thermal effects, or reduce them for an irregular shape.

Expansion joint and seismic joint

In Morocco, the seismic code RPS 2000 (2011 version) adds a requirement: two adjacent blocks must be separated by a seismic joint wide enough to stop them from pounding against each other during an earthquake. The texts set a minimum width of 5 cm, to be increased according to the height of the blocks and the calculated displacements. In practice, the expansion joint is sized to serve as the seismic joint as well. A joint is also recommended between two parts with very different heights or shapes (L-shaped block, a four-storey part adjoining a two-storey part).

Building a joint properly

  • Complete separation of the structure from ground to roof (columns and beams doubled on each side); the foundations can remain shared, unless the soil is heterogeneous.
  • Empty joint: no rubble or mortar stuck inside, otherwise it no longer works.
  • Joint covers on the façade, roof and floor, which allow the movement to occur.
  • Waterproofing treated with a specific upstand on the roof terrace (see roof terrace waterproofing).
  • Services (pipes, cables) fitted with flexible sleeves where they cross the joint.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm divides the building into blocks from the design stage, taking into account the climate, the shape and the RPS, and sizes the width of the joints. Regular cracks along a long building without joints are often a sign that this step was forgotten.

Frequently asked questions

How many metres apart should expansion joints be?

In a hot, dry climate like Marrakech, the BAEL rules lead to about 25 m between joints for a reinforced concrete structure; up to 50 m in a humid, temperate climate.

How wide should a seismic joint be in Morocco?

RPS 2000 (2011 version) sets a minimum width of 5 cm between blocks, to be increased according to the building height and the calculated displacements.

Does a villa need an expansion joint?

A compact villa less than 25 m long generally does not need one, unless it has a very irregular shape or parts of different heights, to be assessed by the engineer.
