Bathroom zones 0, 1, 2: what electrical equipment is allowed?

NF C 15-100 divides the bathroom into zones around the bathtub and shower. The closer you get to water, the stricter the rules.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

The bathroom is divided into three zones: zone 0 (inside the bathtub or shower tray), where almost nothing is allowed except watertight IPX7 12 V safety extra-low voltage (SELV) lighting; zone 1 (above it, up to 2.25 m high), limited to 12 V SELV and certain water heaters; zone 2 (a 60 cm strip around zone 1, up to 2.25 m), where class II light fittings and appliances, IPX4 minimum, are allowed. Beyond that, you are outside the zones and socket outlets are possible.

What is allowed in each zone

ZoneExtentWhat is allowed
Zone 0Inside the bathtub or shower trayOnly 12 V SELV equipment, IPX7 (submersible lighting)
Zone 1Above zone 0, up to 2.25 m from the floor12 V SELV equipment (at least IPX4, IPX5 where there are water jets); electric water heater under conditions
Zone 260 cm around zone 1, up to 2.25 mClass II light fittings and appliances, IPX4 minimum; shaver socket with isolating transformer
Outside the zonesThe rest of the room16 A socket outlets, washing machine, switches, earthed class I appliances

All bathroom circuits are protected by a 30 mA residual current device. Switches are prohibited in zones 0 and 1.

Practical cases

  • Walk-in shower without a fixed screen: zone 1 is no longer limited by the shower tray: it extends over a 1.20 m radius around the fixed shower head, according to the rules of the 2002 edition as amended. So place sockets and switches further away than with a conventional shower tray.
  • Vanity unit with a socket: the socket must be outside the zones, or be limited to a shaver socket in zone 2.
  • Washing machine in the bathroom: outside the zones only.
  • Towel radiator: class II in zone 2, or outside the zones.
  • Electric water heater: preferably outside the zones; in zone 1, only a horizontal appliance placed as high as possible, under the conditions of the standard.

A fixed screen changes everything

A fixed screen, a partition or a ceiling limits the zones: behind a solid screen, you may find yourself outside the zones. This is often the solution for installing a socket near a bathtub in a small bathroom. The space under the bathtub, closed by a panel removable only with a tool, also has its own rules (IPX4 for the equipment allowed there).

Riads, hammams and hotel suites

In the riads and guest houses of Marrakech, tadelakt bathrooms, open showers and private hammams raise specific cases: floor-level mood lighting, illuminated mirrors, hammams with steam generators. A hammam is subject to specific rules. The engineering firm marks out the zones on the plan of each bathroom, chooses the protection ratings and coordinates with the supplementary equipotential bonding. The dimensions given here are those of the 2002 edition of NF C 15-100 as amended; the 2024 edition must be checked for projects that apply it.

Frequently asked questions

Can you have an electrical socket in a bathroom?

Yes, outside the zones, i.e. more than 60 cm from the bathtub or shower (or behind a fixed screen); in zone 2, only a shaver socket with an isolating transformer is allowed.

How wide is zone 2?

Zone 2 forms a 60 cm strip around zone 1, up to a height of 2.25 m, according to the rules of NF C 15-100, 2002 edition as amended.

What lighting can be used in a shower?

In zone 0, only watertight IPX7 12 V SELV lighting is allowed; above, in zone 1, 12 V SELV lighting with at least IPX4, IPX5 where there are water jets.
