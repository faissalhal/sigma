Single-flow or heat recovery ventilation: which to choose in Morocco?

Mechanical ventilation renews the air and removes moisture and odours. Dual-flow ventilation also recovers energy from the extracted air, but it is not justified everywhere.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

In most Moroccan homes, a humidity-controlled single-flow ventilation system is enough: it extracts humid air from wet rooms and adjusts its airflow to humidity. Dual-flow (heat recovery) ventilation is mainly justified in a new, well-insulated and airtight house that is air-conditioned or heated for much of the year, because it recovers a large share of the coolness or warmth of the exhaust air.

How each system works

  • Self-regulating single-flow: a fan extracts air from the kitchen, bathrooms and toilets at a constant rate; fresh air enters through air inlets in the window frames of the dry rooms.
  • Humidity-controlled single-flow: same principle, but the extract grilles or air inlets open more or less depending on humidity. You ventilate more when it is useful and lose less energy the rest of the time.
  • Dual-flow: one network extracts stale air, a second supplies filtered fresh air to the living rooms. The two airflows cross in a heat exchanger without mixing.

Heat exchanger efficiency

According to manufacturers and comparison sites, the exchanger of a dual-flow unit commonly recovers around 70% of the energy, and up to 90% for the best counter-flow models. These values are measured under specific test conditions: in real use, they depend on the airtightness of the duct network and the building. A low-efficiency exchanger offers little more than a good humidity-controlled system.

What the Moroccan climate changes

In Marrakech, the difference between outdoor air and air-conditioned indoor air often exceeds 15 °C in summer. In a continuously air-conditioned villa, dual-flow ventilation limits the intake of hot air and the load on the air conditioner. The filter also traps dust, a real advantage during episodes of dust-laden wind. However:

  • if the house is not very airtight (old windows, riad with an open patio), air passes elsewhere than through the exchanger and the benefit disappears;
  • if the windows remain open for much of the year, the benefit is small;
  • clogged filters reduce airflow: without maintenance, dual-flow ventilation degrades air quality instead of improving it.

A bypass is recommended: it lets cool night air in directly without passing through the exchanger, which is handy in mid-season.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the air inlets or undercutting interior doors too little: air no longer circulates.
  • Running ducts through an overheated roof terrace or loft without insulation.
  • Placing the central unit in an inaccessible room, which makes maintenance impossible.
  • Discharging extracted air too close to the fresh air intake.

How the engineering firm helps you

The engineering firm calculates airflows room by room, chooses the type of ventilation suited to the building's actual airtightness, lays out the network in the false ceilings and coordinates it with the air conditioning. Frequently asked questions

Is dual-flow ventilation useful in Morocco?

Yes in a new, insulated, airtight house that is air-conditioned for long periods, as it recovers about 70 to 90% of the energy of the extracted air; in a building that is not very airtight, a humidity-controlled system is enough.

What is the difference between self-regulating and humidity-controlled ventilation?

Self-regulating ventilation extracts a constant airflow; humidity-controlled ventilation adapts its airflow to room humidity, which ventilates better when needed and wastes less energy.

What maintenance does dual-flow ventilation need?

The filters must be cleaned or changed regularly and the exchanger cleaned; otherwise, airflow drops and air quality deteriorates.
