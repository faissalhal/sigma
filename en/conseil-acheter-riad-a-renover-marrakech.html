Buying a riad to renovate in Marrakech: pitfalls and budget

A riad to renovate in the medina is a dream, but the purchase price is only part of the bill. Here is what to check before signing and how to estimate the work.

Published 16 November 2026 · 4 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Before buying a riad to renovate in Marrakech, check three things: its legal status (land title or melk deed), the actual condition of the load-bearing walls and floors, and the feasibility of the works in the medina (access, permits, neighbours). The renovation budget can equal or exceed the purchase price: it must be estimated before making an offer, not afterwards.

Legal status first

Many riads in the medina are still held as melk, that is, by an adoul deed, without a land title. Buying is possible but riskier: imprecise boundaries, multiple heirs, third-party rights. A titled property can be checked with a certificate of ownership from the land registry; a melk property often requires a lengthy registration procedure, which should be handled with a notary. See our article on land titles and compliance.

Technical points specific to riads

  • Load-bearing walls in rammed earth (pisé), solid brick or rubble stone, often thick and with few tie beams. Vertical cracks at corners, bulging and render coming away should be examined.
  • Timber floors (cedar or eucalyptus beams): insect attack, rot at the bearings, excessive deflection. Some have been replaced with concrete laid on walls that were not designed for it.
  • Party walls: a riad shares its walls with its neighbours. A crack or water ingress may come from the house next door.
  • Damp: rising damp in earth walls, cement renders that stop the wall from drying, a poorly drained patio.
  • Services: electrics and plumbing generally need to be completely redone; the sewer connection is often old.
  • Earthquake: the Al Haouz earthquake of September 2023 weakened part of the old building stock. A structural assessment is essential.

Indicative renovation budget

Level of renovationIndicative range
Light (painting, basic plumbing and electrics)2,500 to 4,500 DH/m²
Intermediate (standard finishes, ordinary zellige)5,000 to 8,000 DH/m²
High-end (fine materials, artisan craftsmanship)up to 16,000 to 25,000 DH/m²

These ranges, published by a property valuation firm, are indicative: they vary greatly with the structural condition. Consolidating walls or replacing floors can on its own push the budget up a category. Add the architect's and engineering firm's fees, and a contingency reserve.

The constraints of building work in the medina

  • Permits: work affecting the structure or façades requires authorisation; the Marrakech medina is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the rules there are stricter. Check with the municipality and the urban agency before committing.
  • Access: narrow derbs, no lorries or concrete mixer trucks, rubble removed by handcart. This makes the works more expensive and longer.
  • Neighbours: propping, work on party walls and disturbance must be planned for.

The role of the engineering firm

Before the offer, an engineer's visit tells you whether the walls and floors can be saved and what strengthening them will cost. During the project, the engineering firm designs the reinforcements, the services and air conditioning suited to the patio. Frequently asked questions

Can you buy a riad without a land title?

Yes, a melk riad (adoul deed) can be bought, but it is riskier: you need to check the chain of ownership and often plan for a registration procedure with a notary.

How much does renovating a riad in Marrakech cost?

As a rough guide, 2,500 to 4,500 DH/m² for a light renovation, 5,000 to 8,000 DH/m² for an intermediate level, and much more for high-end work, excluding major structural repairs.

Do you need a permit to renovate a riad?

Yes, as soon as the work affects the structure, the façades or the volumes. As the medina is a World Heritage Site, check with the municipality and the urban agency before buying.
