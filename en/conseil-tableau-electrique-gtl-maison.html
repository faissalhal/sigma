Distribution board and GTL: location, number of rows, spare capacity

The distribution board and the dwelling technical duct (GTL) bring together the power supply and the protective devices. Their location and size are decided on the drawings, not on site.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

The distribution board must be placed inside the dwelling or in a directly accessible annex room, with its controls between 0.90 m and 1.80 m above the floor (board without a door), and must keep at least 20% free slots for future additions. Under NF C 15-100, it is integrated into a dwelling technical duct (GTL), a column that also houses the communications enclosure and the incoming networks: 600 mm wide in a dwelling of more than 35 m², 450 mm below that.

Key benchmarks

ItemUsual benchmark (NF C 15-100)
Height of controlsBetween 0.90 m and 1.80 m from the floor; with a door, between 0.50 m and 1.80 m
Spare capacityAt least 20% free slots in a detached house, with no empty row
GTL, dwelling of more than 35 m²600 mm wide, about 200 mm deep
GTL, dwelling of 35 m² or less450 mm wide, about 150 mm deep
LocationIn the dwelling or a directly accessible annex room, generally near the entrance or the garage

For a dwelling adapted to people with reduced mobility, the height range is lower (Legrand cites 0.75 m to 1.30 m for the isolating device). These values are those of the 2002 edition as amended; the 2024 edition must be checked for projects that apply it.

How many rows?

The number of rows follows from the number of circuits: each miniature circuit breaker generally takes up one module, each residual current switch two or four modules, and a standard row has 13 or 18 modules. For a villa of 200 to 300 m², with 25 to 40 circuits, 6 to 8 residual current devices, a surge protector and contactors, a 4-row board is commonly needed, or even a main board plus sub-distribution boards per floor or for the pool. Add 20% spare capacity and wiring space: a board that is too small quickly becomes impossible to extend.

Where to place it in a villa?

  • Near the entrance or the garage, accessible without going through a bedroom, and not in a bathroom or above a sink.
  • Away from heat: in Marrakech, a board in an unventilated, west-facing room can exceed 40 °C in summer, which makes the devices heat up and causes tripping.
  • Close to the centre of gravity of the loads to limit cable length and voltage drop, particularly towards the kitchen and the air conditioning units.
  • In a riad: provide a ventilated plant room and one sub-distribution board per floor rather than dozens of cables running through the old walls.

What the GTL contains

  1. The main incoming circuit breaker and the distribution board.
  2. The communications enclosure (telephone, fibre, RJ45, television).
  3. The incoming networks from the meter (Radeema, ONEE or another distributor).
  4. Socket outlets to power the internet box and the equipment in the enclosure.

The engineering firm draws the board on the single-line diagram, counts the modules and places the GTL on the architect's drawings from the design stage, to avoid squeezing it into a cupboard that is too narrow later on. See also the number of residual current devices and dedicated circuits.

Frequently asked questions

At what height should a distribution board be installed?

Under NF C 15-100, the controls of a board without a door are between 0.90 m and 1.80 m from the floor; with a door, the range is 0.50 m to 1.80 m.

How much spare capacity should a distribution board have?

At least 20% free slots in a detached house, without leaving any row completely empty, so that circuits can be added later.

How wide should a GTL be?

600 mm wide and about 200 mm deep for a dwelling of more than 35 m², 450 mm wide for a dwelling of 35 m² or less.
