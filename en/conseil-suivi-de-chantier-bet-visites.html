Site supervision by the engineering consultancy: which visits to plan

A perfect drawing is useless if the site does not follow it. Supervision by the engineering consultancy means being present at the moments when a mistake becomes invisible.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Site supervision by the technical engineering consultancy consists of checking, at key stages, that the contractor carries out the structural and network drawings as planned. For a villa, this generally means around ten to twenty targeted visits, each before an element is poured, buried or rendered. Each visit gives rise to a written report.

The visits that matter

StageWhat the BET checks
Bottom of excavationDepth and soil type consistent with the soil investigation, cleanliness
Reinforcement of footings and ground beamsDiameters, spacing, concrete cover, column starter bars
Columns and tie beamsCross-sections, closely spaced stirrups in critical zones according to RPS 2011
Each floor before pouringBeams, top reinforcement, openings, reservations, electrical conduits
Concealed networks before renderingPlumbing and electrical routes, drainage slopes, pressure tests
Roof terrace waterproofingSlopes, upstands, insulation, drains
CommissioningPanels, residual current devices, earthing, air conditioning, tests

Why these precise moments

A reinforcement error discovered after pouring can only be corrected through costly strengthening, sometimes demolition. A drain pipe with a reverse slope, once tiled over, causes blockages for years. Supervision is only worth something if the contractor notifies the BET 24 to 48 hours before each stage and waits for its approval before pouring. Write it into the contract. For details on checking reinforcement, see checking reinforcement before pouring.

Architect, BET, inspection body: who does what?

  • The architect monitors architectural compliance and issues the certificate of completion of works required for the occupancy permit.
  • The BET monitors the technical execution of its own studies (structure, building services, electrical).
  • The technical inspection body, if appointed, gives an independent opinion for the client and the insurer.

What a good visit report contains

  1. Date, stage visited, people present.
  2. Findings with photos.
  3. Non-conformities and corrections requested.
  4. A clear decision: pouring authorised, authorised after correction, or refused.

What a poorly supervised site costs. The most costly defects seen in expert appraisals often have a simple origin: incomplete reinforcement, insufficient concrete cover, poorly vibrated concrete, drainage without slope, missing earthing. Each would have been corrected in a few minutes before pouring or closing up. Afterwards, you have to investigate, demolish, strengthen and sometimes rehouse the occupants. Supervision represents a small fraction of the construction budget, out of all proportion to such remedial works. It also provides written evidence that is useful if a dispute arises with the contractor, or if the insurer asks how the building was constructed.

The role of the engineering consultancy

Frequently asked questions

How many site visits for a villa?

Generally around ten to twenty targeted visits, at the stages when the work is about to be poured, buried or rendered; the number depends on the size and the number of storeys.

When must the BET absolutely come?

Before each concrete pour (footings, columns, floors) to check the reinforcement, and before concealed networks are closed up.

Is the architect enough to supervise the site?

The architect monitors architectural compliance; technical inspection of the structure and networks is the responsibility of the BET that carried out these studies.
