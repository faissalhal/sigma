Checking reinforcement before pouring: the site checklist

Once the concrete is poured, it is too late: the reinforcement becomes invisible. The inspection before pouring is when the real strength of your structure is decided.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Before each pour of footings, columns, beams or slabs, the reinforcement must be compared point by point with the construction drawings: bar diameters and numbers, stirrup spacing, laps, anchorages, cover and cleanliness. This inspection, carried out by the engineering firm or the technical inspection body, often takes less than an hour and prevents irreparable defects.

The inspection checklist

ItemWhat is checked
Main barsNumber, diameter (HA10, HA12, HA14…) and position as per the drawing
Stirrups and linksDiameter, spacing, closer spacing in critical zones, closed stirrups
LapsSufficient length, staggered and not all at the same place
AnchoragesBeam bars anchored into the columns, starter bars in place for the next floor
Column-beam jointsStirrups present in the joint, despite the difficulty of placing them
CoverSpacers and chairs of the right thickness, in sufficient numbers
CleanlinessBars free of soil, oil and loose rust; formwork cleaned
Openings and sleevesDucts and sleeves provided, without cutting or moving the reinforcement

The most frequent defects

  • Substituted diameters: an HA12 instead of an HA14, due to lack of stock. The steel area drops by nearly 27%.
  • Stirrups too far apart at the ends of columns and beams, where the seismic code requires them to be closer.
  • Poorly formed hooks: seismic rules require stirrups closed by hooks bent into the concrete core, which do not open in an earthquake.
  • Laps too short. When the drawings give no indication, the BAEL gives a standard length of about 50 diameters for FeE500 steel (40 diameters for FeE400).
  • Crushed top mesh in slabs, which loses all effectiveness over supports and cantilevers.
  • Forgotten top bars over beams and slab supports.

How to organise the inspection

Stipulate in the contractor's contract that each pour of a structural element is preceded by acceptance of the reinforcement, with a notice period (for example 24 to 48 hours). The inspector writes a report with photos, and the corrections are checked before the pour is authorised. Dated photos then form a useful archive for handover and in case of dispute. See also our article on site supervision by the engineering firm.

After the inspection: the pour

Inspecting the reinforcement is pointless if the concrete is then poorly placed: added water, no vibration, pouring in high heat without precautions. The inspector also checks the mix or the delivery note of the ready-mixed concrete, and has test cubes or cylinders taken on important elements. On mix design, read our article on concrete mix proportions.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm that designed the structure is best placed to inspect its reinforcement: it knows which details are critical and why. Its visits before pouring turn drawings on paper into a structure that genuinely complies with RPS 2011.

Frequently asked questions

Who should inspect the reinforcement before pouring?

The engineering firm that designed the structure or the appointed technical inspection body. The contractor must notify it in advance of each pour of a load-bearing element.

What lap length for reinforcing bars?

It is shown on the drawings; without a calculation, the BAEL gives a standard value of about 50 diameters for FeE500 steel and 40 diameters for FeE400.

Can an HA14 be replaced by an HA12?

Not without the engineer's approval: the steel area drops from 1.54 to 1.13 cm² per bar, i.e. about 27% less.
