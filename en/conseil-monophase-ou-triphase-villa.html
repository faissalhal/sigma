Single-phase or three-phase for a villa in Morocco: how to choose

The choice between single-phase and three-phase is made when applying for a connection to the distributor. It depends on the villa's total power and on a few large pieces of equipment.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

For a villa in Morocco, single-phase (230 V) is enough as long as the power demand remains modest and no appliance requires three-phase. Three-phase (400 V between phases) becomes relevant when the power exceeds about ten kVA, or when the house has central air conditioning, a pool heat pump, an 11 or 22 kW EV charger or a lift. The exact power threshold above which the distributor requires three-phase should be confirmed with them (ONEE, Radeema in Marrakech, Lydec, Redal or Amendis depending on the city).

What really changes

In single-phase, all the power flows through a single phase conductor. In three-phase, it is spread across three phases. For the same power, the current per conductor is therefore about three times lower, which allows thinner cables and reduces voltage drop over long distances.

Calculation: I = P / 230 in single-phase, I = P / (1.732 × 400) in three-phase, with a balanced load.

When three-phase is required

  • Central air conditioning: large ducted units and VRV/VRF systems above a certain power only exist in three-phase.
  • Swimming pool: high-power heat pump, filtration pump for a large pool, counter-current swimming system.
  • Fast EV charger: 11 kW and 22 kW are three-phase powers; single-phase tops out at 7.4 kW.
  • Lift, large booster pump, workshop: three-phase motors are simpler and more robust.
  • Large villa or guesthouse: when the power assessment exceeds what the distributor accepts in single-phase.

The constraints of three-phase

  • Balancing the phases: single-phase circuits (sockets, lighting, split units) must be spread across the three phases. An overloaded phase trips the incoming circuit breaker while the other two are lightly loaded.
  • Bulkier board: four-pole protective devices, distribution blocks, sometimes a sub-board per floor.
  • Connection and subscription costs: these depend on the distributor and the power requested; ask for the price scale before choosing.
  • Safety: 400 V between phases; boxes and trunking must be labelled and work entrusted to a qualified electrician.

Should you go three-phase "just in case"?

Not necessarily. A medium-sized villa with a few wall-mounted split units, a standard pool and an induction kitchen often works very well in single-phase if the subscribed power is well chosen. On the other hand, if you are planning an extension, a heated pool or an electric vehicle, it is simpler to provide conduits and a board that can evolve from the structural works stage. Changing the connection afterwards involves an application to the distributor and reworking the board.

The role of the design office

The design office draws up the power assessment, decides on the type of supply, spreads the circuits across the phases and sizes the cables taking voltage drop into account. It also prepares the technical information for the connection application to the distributor.

Frequently asked questions

Does a villa need three-phase?

Not always: single-phase is enough for an average villa. Three-phase becomes useful above about ten kVA or with central air conditioning, a large pool heat pump, an 11 or 22 kW charger or a lift.

Does three-phase consume more?

No: consumption depends on the appliances, not on the type of supply. Three-phase spreads the power over three phases, which divides the current per conductor by about three.

Can single-phase appliances be connected to a three-phase supply?

Yes: each single-phase circuit is connected between one phase and neutral. You simply need to distribute the circuits properly to balance the three phases.
