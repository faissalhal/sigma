Orienting your house in a hot climate: the rules for Marrakech

Before any insulation or air conditioning, the way the house sits on its plot determines much of its summer comfort.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

In a hot climate like Marrakech, good orientation means placing the large openings to the south, where they are easy to shade, limiting glazing to the west, the most demanding façade in summer, and organising the house to encourage night-time ventilation. These choices cost almost nothing if they are made at the sketch stage.

What the sun does in Marrakech

Marrakech lies at about 31.6° north latitude. At the summer solstice, the midday sun peaks at about 82° above the horizon: it mainly strikes the roof, and a simple canopy shades a south-facing opening. In the afternoon, on the other hand, it is low on the western horizon, at a time when the outdoor air often exceeds 40 °C: no horizontal canopy can block it.

Layout principles

  1. Buffer rooms to the west: garage, storage, laundry and staircase protect the living spaces.
  2. Living room and bedrooms preferably to the south, east or north.
  3. Cross-ventilation: openings on two opposite façades to expel heat at night, when the outdoor air cools down.
  4. Shaded patio: the principle of riads, it creates a reservoir of cooler air and a chimney effect; vegetation and water enhance it.
  5. Thermal mass: heavy walls well insulated on the outside dampen heat peaks.

When the plot dictates the orientation

On a narrow plot whose main façade faces west, compensate with strong external shading (vertical louvres, mashrabiya, shutters), glazing with a low solar factor and openings set back within the thickness of the wall. See solar shading.

The link with the RTCM

The thermal regulation (decree 2-13-874) takes into account the proportion of glazing on the façades; good orientation makes compliance easier and reduces the air conditioning capacity to be installed. See air conditioning capacity.

Colour and the roof. A light-coloured façade and roof reflect a large share of solar radiation, whereas a dark colour absorbs it and heats up. This is the logic behind traditional whitewashed terraces. Combined with good roof terrace insulation, a light finish limits surface temperature and also protects the waterproofing. In dense urban areas, local regulations sometimes impose a colour (ochre in Marrakech): insulation and shading then have to do the work.

The role of the engineering consultancy

The BET can simulate the solar gains of each façade from the sketch stage and advise the architect on the position of openings, shading and insulation, before choices are fixed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best orientation for a house in Marrakech?

Large south-facing openings shaded by a canopy, few openings to the west, buffer rooms to the west and cross-ventilation to cool the house at night.

Why is the west façade the hottest?

It receives low sun in the afternoon, when the outdoor air is at its hottest, and horizontal shading does not block it.

Is a canopy enough on the south side?

Often yes: in Marrakech, the midday summer sun peaks at around 82°; a modest overhang effectively shades a south-facing opening.
