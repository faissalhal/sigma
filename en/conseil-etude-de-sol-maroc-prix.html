Soil study in Morocco: is it mandatory and how much does it cost?

What a geotechnical study reveals, when to commission it, how much it costs for a villa, and why it is one of the most cost-effective expenses on a building project.

Published 16 November 2026 · 6 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Foundations are the only part of a building that cannot be reworked once the house is built. Sizing them without knowing the soil means choosing between oversizing them "to be safe" and risking settlement and cracks. The soil study, or geotechnical study, lets the engineering firm decide on the basis of real data.

What a soil study contains

  • The nature of the layers encountered (fill, silt, clay, rock), identified through boreholes and in-situ tests.
  • The allowable bearing pressure of the soil, i.e. the load it can carry: this is the key figure for sizing the footings.
  • The recommended type of foundation: isolated or strip footings, raft, or deep foundations if the good soil lies too deep.
  • The presence of water (water table) and any aggressiveness of the soil towards concrete.
  • The expected settlement and the precautions for earthworks.

G1, G2: which mission do you need?

Moroccan laboratories commonly refer to the classification of the French standard NF P 94-500. The G1 mission is a preliminary site study; the G2 mission (preliminary design, then detailed design) provides the precise assumptions for designing the foundations. For a two-storey villa (R+1), in practice it is a G2 that the engineering firm asks for before approving the foundations.

Is it mandatory?

It depends on the municipality, the height of the building and the seismic zone: it is systematically required for large projects, and often required in the permit application. For a villa, even when it is not imposed, it is strongly recommended. Ask the municipality or your engineering firm for the list of documents required for your project.

How much does a soil study cost?

ProjectTypical boreholesIndicative price
Single-storey villa (under 150 m²)2 boreholes to about 6 m5,000 to 7,000 DH
Two-storey villa, R+1 (150 to 300 m²)2 to 3 boreholes to about 8 m6,000 to 10,000 DH
Small building, hotel, riad with basementDepending on the programmeOn quotation, often above 15,000 DH

These ranges, collected from Moroccan laboratories, vary with site access and the number of tests. Compared with the cost of a villa, they are a tiny fraction of the budget, whereas an unsuitable foundation can cost tens of thousands of dirhams in remedial work.

The right time

Before the reinforced concrete studies, ideally as soon as the building's position on the plot is known. The laboratory report is the engineering firm's first input for the load calculation and the choice of foundations.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a soil study cost for a villa in Morocco?

Generally between 5,000 and 10,000 DH for a villa, depending on the floor area, the number of boreholes and site access.

Is a soil study mandatory in Morocco?

It is required for large projects and often in the permit application; for a villa, it is strongly recommended even when it is not imposed.

Which geotechnical mission for a house?

A G2 (design) mission is generally the one the engineering firm asks for to size the foundations of a villa.
