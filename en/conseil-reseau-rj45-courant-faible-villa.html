Villa low-voltage systems: RJ45, communication enclosure, fibre

Wi-Fi does not replace cable. In a reinforced concrete villa, with several floors and thick walls, a well-designed RJ45 network planned from the structural works stage avoids years of unstable connections.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

For a new villa, the basis is a communication enclosure placed near the distribution board, from which 4-pair twisted cables of grade 2TV or 3TV run in a star layout to RJ45 sockets in each main room (at least two in the living room), plus dedicated points for equipment: Wi-Fi access points, cameras, intercom, televisions. The operator's fibre entry ends in this enclosure. These benchmarks come from NF C 15-100, applied by design offices in Morocco.

The components of a villa network

  • Entry duct: a dedicated conduit from the property boundary to the enclosure, for the operator's fibre (Maroc Telecom, Orange, inwi depending on the area).
  • Communication enclosure: it houses the router, a patch panel, a switch and a power supply. Provide a power socket nearby and ventilation: in summer, a closed plant room in Marrakech easily exceeds the temperatures routers can withstand.
  • Star wiring: one cable per socket, directly from the enclosure, with no daisy-chaining from one socket to another.
  • RJ45 sockets: they can be used interchangeably for internet, TV via the router or telephone.

Grade 2TV or grade 3TV?

GradeTarget data rateUse
Grade 1Telephone and basic dataOld standard, to be avoided in new builds
Grade 2TV1 Gb/s and televisionUsual minimum for a new home
Grade 3TV10 Gb/s and television, including satelliteHigh-end villa, professional use, longevity

The price difference between the two is small compared with the cost of installation; for a new villa, grade 3TV is a good choice. The cable laid today will be in use for twenty or thirty years.

How many sockets and where?

  • Living room: two RJ45 sockets side by side near the television, another near any desk.
  • Each bedroom: at least one socket, two if a desk or television is planned.
  • Office: two to four sockets.
  • Ceilings of corridors or landings: one socket for a Wi-Fi access point powered by the cable (PoE), on each floor.
  • Outdoor areas: PoE surveillance cameras, video intercom at the gate, Wi-Fi access point for the pool.

Installation rules

  • Separate conduits from power cables, to avoid interference, and crossings at right angles.
  • Bend radii respected and reasonable lengths (the Ethernet standard limits a link to 90 m of fixed cable).
  • Labelling of each cable at both ends and testing of each link at handover.
  • Spare conduits to the gate, the pool house and camera points, laid during the structural works and external services.

The role of the design office

The design office includes low-voltage systems in the same file as the power installation: socket layout, location and size of the enclosure, cable routes, Wi-Fi coverage, video surveillance, access control, alarm and, for a riad run as a guesthouse or a hotel, the wiring for fire detection. Planning all this on drawings costs far less than running surface cables afterwards. For all the sockets in the house, see the number of sockets per room.

Frequently asked questions

What cable for an RJ45 network in a house?

A 4-pair twisted cable of grade 2TV at minimum, or grade 3TV to target 10 Gb/s and longevity, laid in a star layout from the communication enclosure.

How many RJ45 sockets should be planned?

At least one per main room and two in the living room, plus dedicated points for Wi-Fi access points, cameras and the intercom.

Where should the communication enclosure go?

Near the distribution board, in a ventilated location with a power socket, where the operator's fibre duct arrives.
