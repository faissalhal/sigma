Rainwater harvesting in Morocco: is it worth it for your house?

In Marrakech, rainfall is low and irregular. Harvesting rainwater is still useful for irrigation, provided the tank is sized realistically.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Yes, rainwater harvesting can be useful in Morocco, but it has to be calculated honestly. In Marrakech, published averages are around 200 to 275 mm of rain per year, concentrated between October and April, with large variations from one year to the next. A 150 m² roof can thus collect around 25 to 30 m³ per year: enough to water part of the garden, not to supply the whole house.

The calculation in three lines

1 mm of rain falling on 1 m² gives 1 litre of water. The recoverable volume is therefore: roof area (m²) × annual rainfall (mm) × collection coefficient (about 0.8 for a flat roof, allowing for evaporation, the first flush and overflows).

400 m² (large villa)about 48 m³about 80 m³

Since the rain falls in a few episodes, the tank must be able to store a heavy downpour: in practice a few cubic metres for a house, more for a villa with a garden. A huge tank that never fills up is a poor investment.

What rainwater can be used for

  • Watering the garden and plants: the simplest and most cost-effective use.
  • Cleaning outdoor floors and vehicles.
  • Toilet flushing: possible with a separate, clearly marked network, with no connection whatsoever to the drinking water network.
  • Not for drinking or cooking without treatment and health checks: the water runs off a terrace exposed to dust and birds.

The components of a good installation

  • A clean terrace and accessible rainwater downpipes, with a leaf guard or grille.
  • A filter or first-flush diverter: the first rain washes the terrace and carries off the dust accumulated over the summer.
  • A buried or shaded tank, opaque and closed: the water stays cool, free of algae and mosquitoes.
  • An overflow connected to the stormwater network or to a soakaway, and a non-return valve.
  • A pump with a filter if the water is to be distributed under pressure (see booster pump).
  • Complete separation from the drinking water network: if the tank is topped up with mains water, the top-up is done through a free air gap, never through a direct connection.

A buried tank is a structure

A concrete tank is designed as a buried structure (earth pressure, vehicles on the surface, watertightness). A prefabricated tank must follow the manufacturer's installation instructions. In all cases, rainwater harvesting is studied together with the plot's drainage and stormwater.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm compares the available resource with actual needs (garden area, type of planting, drip irrigation), chooses the tank volume, designs the separate network and coordinates the tank with the structure and the external works. In the Marrakech climate, combining rainwater harvesting, low-water plants and drip irrigation gives better results than an oversized tank.

Frequently asked questions

How much rainwater can be collected in Marrakech?

With about 200 to 275 mm of rain per year, a 150 m² roof collects around 25 to 30 m³ per year, depending on the year and the losses.

Can harvested rainwater be drunk?

Not without treatment and health checks; it is used for watering, cleaning and possibly toilet flushing through a separate network.

What size of tank is needed to harvest rainwater?

A few cubic metres are often enough for a house; the volume is calculated from the roof area, the rainfall and the watering needs.
