Damp, condensation and mould in the home: causes and solutions

Black stains in the corners, misted windows, a musty smell: before repainting, you need to know where the water comes from. Condensation is only one of the possible causes.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Mould appears when a surface stays damp for a long time. The most common cause in an occupied house is condensation: the water vapour produced by the occupants settles on cold surfaces for lack of sufficient ventilation. But a wall can also be wetted by water ingress or by rising damp. The treatment depends entirely on the diagnosis.

The right humidity level

Reference bodies, including ADEME in France, recommend indoor relative humidity of between 40 and 60%. Above 60% on a lasting basis, the risk of condensation, mould and dust mites increases significantly; beyond 70%, there is a real problem to deal with. A hygrometer costing a few dozen dirhams lets you monitor this level room by room.

Why condensation forms

Air contains water vapour. When it touches a surface colder than its dew-point temperature, this vapour turns back into liquid. A family produces several litres of vapour every day (breathing, showers, cooking, laundry drying). If this vapour is not removed, it settles on the coldest points: single glazing, wall corners, slab edges and uninsulated concrete columns. In summer, the opposite happens: condensation can appear on uninsulated cold water pipes or air conditioning ducts.

Lasting solutions

  • Ventilate: extraction in the bathroom and kitchen, air inlets in dry rooms; humidity-controlled mechanical ventilation is the most reliable solution.
  • Use the cooker hood while cooking and air the room after showering.
  • Treat thermal bridges: insulating columns, beams and slab edges on the façade (see our article on thermal bridges).
  • Heat steadily in winter rather than in bursts: the walls stay warmer.
  • Do not block the vents, and do not place large furniture flush against a cold exterior wall.
  • Clean the mould with a suitable product, then treat the cause; repainting without changing anything only delays the return of the stains.

In old houses and riads, walls in rammed earth, stone or solid brick need to "breathe". Cement renders and impermeable paints trap moisture and make the damage worse. It is often necessary to go back to lime renders and treat rising damp at the source.

Diagnosis by an engineering firm

The engineering firm measures the moisture in the walls and the air, identifies thermal bridges, checks the waterproofing and ventilation, then proposes prioritised works. This diagnosis avoids spending money on pointless treatments and repainting several times.

Frequently asked questions

What is the ideal humidity level in a house?

Between 40 and 60% relative humidity; above 60% on a lasting basis, the risk of condensation and mould increases significantly.

How can you tell whether it is condensation or water ingress?

Condensation causes misting and stains in cold corners, especially in winter; water ingress causes a localised stain that gets worse after rain, often under a terrace or around a window or door frame.

Is painting with anti-damp paint enough?

No, it hides the problem without treating it; the cause must be removed first (ventilation, thermal bridge, waterproofing or rising damp).
