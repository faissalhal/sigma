Basements: tanking, waterproofing and water ingress

A basement that lets in water is one of the most expensive defects to put right. Protection is decided at the design stage, according to the water present in the ground.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

To protect a basement, the solution depends on the water it will face: simple drainage with a damp-proofing render may be enough in dry, free-draining soil; where there is a water table or water under pressure, tanking is needed, i.e. a watertight envelope able to resist water pressure. The most widely used technical reference is the French DTU 14.1, revised in 2020.

Knowing the water on your site

Everything starts with the soil investigation: level of the water table and its variations, soil permeability, risk of surface runoff or leaking pipes. In Marrakech, the water table is often deep, but basements are exposed to garden irrigation water, neighbouring swimming pools, pipe leaks and violent storms. A villa basement next to a swimming pool or a heavily watered garden must be treated as exposed to water.

The three families of tanking

TypePrincipleUse
Watertight concrete structureThe reinforced concrete itself provides watertightness: controlled thickness, reinforcement and cracking, treated jointsCar parks, rooms that tolerate slight dampness
Damp-proofing coatingWater-repellent render or mortar applied on the inside, bonded to the concretePlant rooms, cellars, refurbishment
Waterproofing membraneContinuous membrane, generally on the outside, protectedHabitable rooms requiring perfect watertightness

The choice depends on how the room will be used: a car park tolerates a few traces of damp, a bedroom, a home cinema or an electrical plant room does not.

Weak points to take care of

  • Construction joints between the raft and the walls, and between wall lifts: waterstops or water-bar strips.
  • Penetrations for pipes and cables: watertight sleeves provided before pouring.
  • Formwork tie holes and spacers, to be filled carefully.
  • Cracking of the concrete, limited by the reinforcement and proper curing.
  • Perimeter drainage and discharge of rainwater away from the buried walls.

What to do in case of water ingress?

First identify the source: rising water table, runoff, pipe leak, local defect (construction joint, penetration) or condensation. Local leaks are often treated by injecting resin into the crack or joint. Widespread ingress requires internal tanking that resists water pressure, or external drainage if the ground is accessible. A simple waterproof render on a wall subject to water under pressure generally ends up peeling off. Also remember to ventilate the basement: part of the damp observed often comes from condensation on cold walls.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm chooses the type of tanking with the architect according to the use, sizes the raft and walls for earth and water pressure, and details the joints and penetrations. Buried walls are also retaining walls, and the raft is part of the foundation choice.

Frequently asked questions

What is tanking?

It is a watertight envelope that protects a buried room against water, including water under pressure: watertight reinforced concrete, a damp-proofing coating or a waterproofing membrane.

How do you stop water ingress in a basement?

First find its source; a local leak is often treated by injection, widespread ingress by internal tanking or external drainage.

Does a basement in Marrakech need tanking?

Not always, as the water table is often deep, but water from irrigation, swimming pools and leaks can reach the buried walls. The soil investigation and the use of the room decide.
