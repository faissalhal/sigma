Septic tank in Morocco: sizing, installation and maintenance

Outside areas connected to the sewer, a villa or a farm must treat its own wastewater. A properly sized and maintained tank prevents smells, overflows and pollution of the well.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

For a single-family house, the most widely used benchmark is that of "all-waters" septic tanks: 3 m³ of working volume up to 5 main rooms (bedrooms plus living room and kitchen counted according to the manufacturer's rule), then 1 m³ more per additional room. The tank alone is not enough: it must be followed by a treatment stage (drainage field, sand filter or planted filter) and emptied when the sludge reaches about half the volume.

How a septic tank works

The tank receives the wastewater from the house. Heavy matter settles at the bottom (the sludge), grease floats on the surface, and an initial fermentation breaks down part of the pollution. The water that leaves it is still loaded: it must be purified by the soil or by a filter before being dispersed. A tank that discharges directly into a soakaway pit or a wadi pollutes the groundwater.

These values come from French practice for all-waters tanks and from manufacturers' data sheets. In Morocco, the technical guides of ONEE and the Ministry of Equipment for on-site sanitation size the tank according to the number of population equivalents. For a building open to the public or a guesthouse, the calculation is based on the actual number of people and flow rates, and must be accepted by the authorities when the building permit is issued.

Installation rules

  • Distance: away from the well or borehole, trees and property boundaries, and lower than the well in the direction of groundwater flow. The required distances should be checked with the municipality or the river basin agency.
  • Access: the inspection covers must remain accessible to the emptying truck; do not tile over them.
  • Ventilation: an air inlet through the primary ventilation and a high-level gas outlet, otherwise there will be smells and corrosion of the concrete.
  • Rainwater: never into the tank; it saturates it and flushes the sludge towards the drainage field.
  • Prefabricated or masonry tank: a prefabricated tank offers controlled watertightness; a masonry tank must be watertight and designed as a buried structure (earth pressure, groundwater).

Maintenance

  • Empty when the sludge takes up about 50% of the working volume: often every 3 to 5 years for a family, to be adjusted according to use.
  • Do not pour in cooking grease, wipes, chlorinated products in large quantities, or used engine oil.
  • Check the pre-filter or grease trap if there is one.

Septic tank or sewer connection?

If a public network runs nearby, connection is generally the preferred solution; if your plot is lower than the sewer, a sewage lifting station may be needed. In areas without a network, such as many plots on the outskirts of Marrakech, the engineering firm sizes the complete treatment system based on the soil survey (permeability, groundwater level) and includes it in the permit application.

Frequently asked questions

What size septic tank for a house?

The usual benchmark is 3 m³ of working volume up to 5 main rooms, plus 1 m³ per additional room; beyond that, or for a guesthouse, a specific study is needed.

How often should a septic tank be emptied?

When the sludge reaches about 50% of the working volume, which is often every 3 to 5 years for a family, depending on use.

Can rainwater be sent into the septic tank?

No: rainwater saturates the tank and carries the sludge into the drainage field, which then clogs. It must have its own drainage.
