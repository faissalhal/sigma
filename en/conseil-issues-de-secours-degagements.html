Emergency exits and escape routes: number, width, exit units

How many exits, how wide, and where to place them: the calculation logic for the escape routes of a restaurant, a shop or a hotel.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

The number and width of emergency exits depend on the occupancy the space can receive. Width is counted in exit units (UP) of 0.60 m, with a rounding rule: a one-unit exit actually measures 0.90 m and a two-unit exit 1.40 m. These rules, taken from French ERP regulations, are the most widespread calculation reference; in Morocco, the values to apply are those of the fire safety regulation (Decree 2-14-499), to be checked for each project.

Benchmarks by occupancy

Occupancy of the spaceEscape routes (French regulation benchmarks)
1 to 19 people1 escape route of 0.90 m
20 to 50 people2 escape routes: one of 0.90 m and a second of 0.60 m (or an ancillary escape route)
51 to 100 people2 escape routes of 0.90 m, or 1 of 1.40 m and 1 of 0.60 m
101 to 200 people1 escape route of 1.40 m and 1 of 0.90 m
Above that1 additional UP per 100 people, and more exits

Not all guides present these cases in exactly the same way: use this table to understand the logic and have the calculation validated against the applicable text.

How occupancy is calculated

Occupancy is determined by activity: number of seats in a restaurant, density of people per m² of public area in a shop, number of beds in a hotel, plus the staff. It is the first figure to set: an error at this stage skews everything else.

The rules that go with the width

  • Exits far apart from one another, so that a single fire does not block them all.
  • Limited travel distances to an exit or a protected staircase, and reduced dead-end length.
  • Doors opening in the direction of escape above a certain occupancy, operable without a key from the inside, with a panic bar where required.
  • Staircases sized like the escape routes, often protected and with smoke control.
  • Signage with illuminated emergency luminaires: see our article on emergency lighting.
  • No obstacles: no storage, no goods, no locked door during operation.

Common pitfalls in Marrakech

  1. The riad converted into a restaurant or guesthouse with a single staircase and a single door onto the derb.
  2. The roof terrace fitted out as a bar, accessible only by the main staircase.
  3. The shop whose secondary exit leads into a locked storeroom.
  4. Fixed security grilles on windows that could serve as an exit.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm calculates the occupancy, sizes and positions the escape routes from the sketch stage and proposes solutions when the existing building does not allow the rule to be applied to the letter (limiting occupancy, an additional staircase, compensatory measures). Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum width of an emergency exit?

A one-unit exit measures 0.90 m and a two-unit exit 1.40 m, following the logic of French ERP regulations, widely used as a reference.

How many exits for 50 people?

According to French regulation benchmarks, two escape routes, one of 0.90 m and a second of 0.60 m or an ancillary escape route; to be checked against the applicable Moroccan text.

What is an exit unit?

It is the width needed for one file of people to pass, i.e. 0.60 m; the total width of the escape routes is calculated as a number of exit units according to occupancy.
