Oven and hob: which cable and which circuit breaker to choose?

The kitchen concentrates the most powerful appliances in the house. Here are the usual cable cross-sections and circuit breaker ratings for the oven and hob, and frequent mistakes on site.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

In single-phase, the hob (or cooker) is supplied by a dedicated 6 mm² circuit protected by a 32 A circuit breaker, placed downstream of a 30 mA type A residual current switch. The oven also has its own circuit, commonly 2.5 mm² protected by a 20 A circuit breaker. In three-phase, the hob can be supplied in 2.5 mm² with a 20 A four-pole circuit breaker. These are the NF C 15-100 benchmarks, the reference most used by engineering firms in Morocco.

The usual values

HobThree-phase 400 V2.5 mm² (5 conductors)20 A
Built-in ovenSingle-phase 230 V2.5 mm²20 A
Hood, microwave, small appliancesSingle-phase 230 VKitchen socket circuit 2.5 mm²20 A

Some hob manufacturers or certain manuals specify other ratings for three-phase or two-phase connection: the appliance manual and the engineering firm's calculation note take precedence over generic tables.

Connection: cable outlet or socket

  • Hob: connection via a connection box (cable outlet) or a dedicated 32 A socket, placed so that it remains accessible under the worktop.
  • Oven: dedicated 16 A or 20 A socket, or cable outlet depending on the model. A socket placed behind the oven must be possible to disconnect by pulling the appliance out.
  • Residual current device: the hob circuit must be under a 30 mA type A residual current switch, because induction hobs generate leakage currents that type AC does not detect correctly.

Mistakes to avoid

  1. Plugging the hob into a 16 A socket with an extension lead or adapter: risk of overheating and fire.
  2. Putting the oven and the hob on the same 32 A circuit: each appliance must have its own circuit, except an all-in-one cooker.
  3. Undersizing a long cable: if the kitchen is far from the board (single-storey villa, summer kitchen near the pool), voltage drop may require a larger cross-section.
  4. Forgetting three-phase: in a villa with a three-phase connection, supplying the hob in three-phase spreads the load better between the phases.

The engineering firm's view

In a villa or a high-end apartment, the kitchen may include an induction hob, two ovens, a dishwasher, an American-style refrigerator and a wine cooler. The engineering firm includes these loads in the electrical power balance, chooses the cross-sections, distributes the circuits between the residual current devices and checks the power to be contracted with the distributor. See also mandatory dedicated circuits and the cable cross-section / rating table.

Frequently asked questions

What cable size for an induction hob?

In single-phase, the hob is supplied in 6 mm² with a 32 A circuit breaker, under a 30 mA type A residual current device; in three-phase, 2.5 mm² with a 20 A circuit breaker is the usual value.

Which circuit breaker for an electric oven?

The oven has a dedicated circuit, commonly 2.5 mm² protected by a 20 A circuit breaker, unless the manufacturer's manual states otherwise.

Can the oven and hob be on the same circuit breaker?

No, except for an all-in-one cooker: NF C 15-100 provides for one dedicated circuit for the hob and another for the oven.
