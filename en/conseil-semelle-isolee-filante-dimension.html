Pad or strip footing dimensions: how to calculate them

The size of a footing follows from a calculation that is simple in principle: the load it receives divided by what the soil can bear. The rest is a matter of reinforcement and detailing.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

The area of a footing is calculated by dividing the load transmitted by the column or wall by the allowable bearing pressure of the soil given by the geotechnical investigation. There is therefore no "standard size": the same villa may need footings 1 m wide on rocky soil and nearly 2 m wide on poor soil.

The principle of the calculation

Take a column bringing down 300 kN (about 30 tonnes) onto a soil with an allowable bearing pressure of 2 bars (0.2 MPa, i.e. 200 kN/m²):

  • Required area: 300 / 200 = 1.5 m².
  • Square footing: square root of 1.5 ≈ 1.22 m, rounded up to 1.25 × 1.25 m.
  • On a 1-bar soil, the same load would require 3 m², i.e. about 1.75 × 1.75 m.

For a strip footing, the reasoning is the same per linear metre of wall: the load per metre divided by the allowable pressure gives the width. The engineer adds the self-weight of the footing and the soil above, and the load combinations, including earthquake according to RPS 2000 (2011 version).

The depth of the footing

A footing must be thick enough to transmit the column force to its whole area without failing. In the classic "strut" method of the BAEL, the effective depth is at least a quarter of the projection, i.e. (B - b) / 4, B being the width of the footing and b that of the column. With a 1.25 m footing under a 25 cm column, this gives at least 25 cm of effective depth, plus the concrete cover. The exact values and the reinforcement (bottom mesh in both directions, column starter bars) are to be determined by the engineer.

Points to respect on site

  • The founding level given by the soil investigation: you dig down to the good soil, even if it is deeper than expected.
  • The blinding concrete, a layer of a few centimetres of lean concrete that protects the bottom of the excavation and supports the reinforcement.
  • The concrete cover of the bars, greater for concrete cast against the ground (see reinforcement cover).
  • The tie beams connecting the footings to each other, required by the seismic code.
  • Centring the column on the footing: an eccentric footing (on a property boundary, for example) is designed differently.

Common mistakes

Copying the footing sizes from a neighbour, reducing their size to save concrete, or placing a footing on uncompacted fill are the classic causes of settlement and cracks. An undersized footing can only be fixed by costly underpinning.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm establishes the load take-down column by column, sizes each footing according to the actual soil and provides a dimensioned foundation plan with reinforcement. It then inspects the bottom of the excavation and the reinforcement before pouring.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the area of a footing?

Divide the load transmitted by the column by the allowable bearing pressure of the soil: 300 kN on a 2-bar soil (200 kN/m²) requires 1.5 m², i.e. a footing of about 1.25 × 1.25 m.

How thick should a pad footing be?

According to the strut method, the effective depth is at least a quarter of the projection, (B - b) / 4. The exact value and the reinforcement are set by the engineer.

Can footings be built without a soil investigation?

It is not advisable: without a measured allowable bearing pressure, the engineer must use a cautious assumption, and an error about the soil is the leading cause of settlement.
