AC outdoor unit noise: where to install it without disturbing?

Noise from outdoor units is one of the leading causes of neighbour disputes related to air conditioning. A good location, chosen from the drawings stage, solves most of the problem.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

An air conditioning outdoor unit generally emits between about 45 and 65 dB(A) depending on its capacity and quality. The perceived level depends above all on where it is placed: against a wall, in a corner or in a small courtyard, the sound is reflected and amplified. Placed on an open terrace, on anti-vibration mounts and away from bedroom windows, the same machine becomes discreet.

Why location matters as much as the unit

Hard surfaces reflect noise. According to specialist installers, the approximate figures are:

Unit positionEffect on the sound level (order of magnitude)
Free field (open terrace, garden)Reference
Against a wallAbout +3 dB
In the corner of two wallsAbout +6 dB
In an enclosed courtyard or patioAbout +9 dB

Since the decibel scale is logarithmic, +3 dB corresponds to a doubling of sound energy. The patios, narrow alleys and small courtyards of the medinas are therefore the worst locations: sound bounces around and rises towards every window.

Good installation rules

  • Keep the unit away from bedroom windows, both yours and your neighbours', and avoid party walls.
  • Direct the airflow towards an open space (garden, wide street), never towards a neighbour's window.
  • Decouple: anti-vibration pads or mounts, a support on a slab rather than fixed to a lightweight partition, flexible connections.
  • Respect the manufacturer's clearances: a unit that recirculates its own hot air works harder and makes more noise.
  • Choose an inverter unit: most of the time it runs at reduced, quieter speed, instead of starting at full power.
  • Compare the data sheets: the sound power or sound pressure level is stated by manufacturers; a difference of a few dB can be heard.

Solutions when the noise is already a nuisance

  • Move the unit to the flat roof, often the best option in town.
  • Install an acoustic screen or enclosure designed for air conditioning, which lets air through (a closed box makes the machine overheat).
  • Replace rigid supports with anti-vibration mounts.
  • Have the unit checked: an unbalanced fan or a worn compressor makes more noise.

Neighbours and co-ownership

In an apartment building, installing a unit on the façade or in common areas is generally governed by the co-ownership rules and requires the agreement of the property manager; some municipalities also regulate the appearance of façades, particularly in the medina. In France, the criterion used for neighbourhood noise is the emergence above ambient noise (5 dB(A) during the day, 3 dB(A) at night); there is no fixed legal distance. In Morocco, it is better to plan ahead: a considerate installation avoids disputes.

Planning ahead from the design stage

In a villa or a riad, the engineering firm chooses the location of the outdoor units at the same time as the system (a VRV or a multi-split reduces the number of machines, see our comparison split, ducted or VRV), plans the bases and the routes for the lines, and checks that the terrace can carry the loads. In a riad, it is often the terrace that houses all the units, away from the patio.

Frequently asked questions

How many decibels does an air conditioning outdoor unit make?

Generally between about 45 and 65 dB(A) depending on capacity and model; the perceived level depends a great deal on the location.

Where should the outdoor unit be placed to limit noise?

In a free field, for example on the flat roof, away from bedroom windows and party walls, on anti-vibration mounts; avoid corners and enclosed courtyards, which amplify sound.

Can a noise enclosure be fitted on an air conditioner?

Yes, provided you use a ventilated acoustic enclosure designed for this purpose; a closed box prevents the unit from releasing its heat and makes it overheat.
