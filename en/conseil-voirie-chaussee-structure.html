Road pavement structure: layers, thicknesses and mistakes to avoid

A road that deforms after two winters almost always has a defect beneath the asphalt. Here is how a pavement is built and what needs to be checked.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

A pavement is a stack of layers that spread vehicle loads down to the ground. From bottom to top, there is the formation (the excavated ground), an optional capping layer, the sub-base, the base course and the wearing course. Its thickness is not chosen at random: it depends on the traffic (especially heavy vehicles) and on the bearing capacity of the soil, measured by laboratory tests.

The layers and their role

LayerCommon materialRole
Formation and capping layerCompacted in-situ soil, selected material, sometimes treated with lime or cementProvide a uniform, load-bearing support and protect the soil from water
Sub-baseUnbound granular sub-base material (GNF)Spread the loads onto the soil
Base courseUnbound granular base material (GNB) or bitumen-bound gravelTake most of the stresses
Wearing courseAsphalt or surface dressingWatertightness, skid resistance, comfort

As an order of magnitude, a low-traffic subdivision access road often has around twenty centimetres of sub-base, fifteen to twenty centimetres of base and a few centimetres of asphalt. These figures are indicative: only a design based on the geotechnical study and the expected traffic can set the thicknesses for your project. For this, engineers rely on catalogues of standard structures and recognised calculation methods.

What makes a pavement last

  • Compaction of each layer, checked by tests (in-situ density, plate load tests). A poorly compacted layer settles under traffic, causing ruts and cracks.
  • Water: it is the main enemy. A crossfall of around 2%, well-set kerbs and gutters and enough gullies prevent water from ponding and seeping into the layers.
  • Trenches: backfilling a utility trench with the excavated soil, without compacting it in layers, creates a weak strip that subsides under the pavement.
  • Heat: in Marrakech, asphalt surface temperatures in summer are very high; the choice of binder and the quality of laying limit rutting.

Checks to plan on site

  1. Acceptance of the formation before placing the first layer.
  2. Checking the materials (grading, cleanliness) on delivery.
  3. Checking compaction and thicknesses layer by layer.
  4. Checking gradients and levels before the wearing course.
  5. Checking the asphalt: laying temperature, thickness, evenness.

These checks are often carried out by a laboratory, at the request of the client or of the municipality at the acceptance of a subdivision.

The role of the engineering firm

The VRD engineering firm uses the soil study, designs the structure, draws the longitudinal and cross profiles, coordinates the road with the underground networks and follows the tests through to acceptance. It is also the one that weighs up a thicker structure against soil treatment, which is often more economical.

Frequently asked questions

What layers make up a road pavement?

The formation and capping layer, then a sub-base, a base course and a wearing course in asphalt or surface dressing.

What thickness for a subdivision road?

It depends on the traffic and the bearing capacity of the soil; an access road often has around twenty centimetres of sub-base, 15 to 20 cm of base and a few centimetres of asphalt, as an indication only.

Why does a new road subside?

Most often because of insufficient compaction, a poorly backfilled trench or water seeping into the layers for lack of gradient and drainage.
