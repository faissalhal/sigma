Cost of building a villa in Marrakech in 2026: price per m²

How much a built m² costs by standard, how much the structural shell weighs, and what to add to the building price to get a realistic budget.

Published 9 November 2026 · 7 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

"How much per m²?" is the first question from almost all our clients. The honest answer is a range, and it has widened: the Moroccan builders' and architects' websites we compared in November 2026 quote differences of up to three times for the same city. Here is how to read these figures and build a budget that holds.

Price ranges per m² in 2026

The values below are including VAT, per m² of covered floor area, building only: excluding land, swimming pool and outdoor works, and fees. These are the ranges we use in our costing tool after cross-checking the sources published for Marrakech.

StandardIndicative price (DH incl. VAT / m²)What it covers
Economy3,500 – 5,000Ordinary structure, standard tiling, basic aluminium joinery, basic fittings
Standard (mid-range)5,000 – 7,500Careful finishes, partial false ceilings, split air conditioning, branded sanitaryware
High-end8,000 – 12,000Large glazed openings, marble or stone, central air conditioning, home automation, bespoke joinery

To put these values in context: EnginLoc publishes a standard of 4,200 to 6,200 DH/m² excluding VAT for Marrakech, i.e. about 5,000 to 7,400 including VAT. AE Architectes places the standard range higher, between 6,000 and 9,000 DH/m², excluding pool and outdoor works. WALL Construction puts high-end turnkey between 8,500 and 12,000 DH/m² including VAT. The differences come mainly from three things: the tax basis (excluding or including VAT, rarely specified), the scope (pool and outdoor works included or not) and the level of finish.

Before comparing two quotes or two articles, always check: excluding or including VAT? Pool and outdoor works included? Fees included?

The structural shell: about 35 to 40% of the budget

The structural shell (earthworks, foundations, reinforced concrete, masonry, ground slab) remains the largest item for a villa. Depending on the source, it represents 30 to 60% of the total cost: the more modest the finishes, the larger the share. For a standard villa, we use about 37 to 40%; for high-end, the share drops towards 30% because the finishes weigh more.

In value terms, the Rifaa.ma simulator gives about 2,300 DH/m² excluding VAT for the structural shell of a 300 m² two-storey (R+1) villa with solid slabs, and WALL Construction quotes 2,400 to 2,800 DH/m² including VAT for the bare structure alone. These amounts depend directly on structural choices: nature of the soil, type of foundations, spans, any basement. This is where the BET's reinforced concrete study saves money, by sizing as precisely as possible without sacrificing safety.

What the price per m² does not include

  • The pool and outdoor works: for a 400 m² luxury villa, AE Architectes puts them at 200,000 – 400,000 DH for the pool and 200,000 – 500,000 DH for the outdoor works.
  • Fees: architect, technical engineering firm (structure, electrical, plumbing, HVAC), inspection body, surveyor, laboratory.
  • Taxes and connections: construction tax, water, electricity and sewerage connections.
  • Contingencies: allow 5 to 10% depending on the complexity of the plot and the programme.

The type of project changes everything

A villa is not a terraced house, let alone a hotel. For the same floor area, a villa has more façades, more bathrooms, often a pool and heavier technical equipment. A hotel or a guest riad adds fire safety, ventilation and centralised hot water. This is why our tool applies a coefficient by type of project rather than a single price.

An upward trend

In October and November 2026, the Moroccan business press reported a 15 to 30% rise in material prices, putting pressure on affordable housing. AE Architectes, for its part, estimates that costs rose by 35 to 50% between 2020 and 2026. A budget drawn up two years ago must therefore be updated before signing.

Our method for a reliable cost estimate

A ratio per m² is enough to know whether a project is feasible. To decide, you need a quantity take-off: the actual quantities taken from the plans, multiplied by updated unit prices, trade by trade. This is what our costing tool does, and what we then refine with the construction drawings.
