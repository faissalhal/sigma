Double glazing against the heat in Morocco: what really matters

Against the heat, ordinary double glazing is not enough: it is above all the solar factor and external shading that make the difference.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Yes, double glazing is useful in Morocco, but not for the reason you might think. It reduces heat exchange by conduction (U-value), which matters in winter and when the temperature difference is large. Against the summer sun, what matters is the solar factor g: the proportion of solar energy that passes through the glass. Clear double glazing still lets most of the radiation through; you need solar control glass or external shading.

The two figures to ask for

Type of glazingU (W/m².K, order of magnitude)Solar factor g (order of magnitude)
Clear single glazingabout 5.8high (close to 0.85)
Clear double glazing 4/16/4about 2.8 to 3about 0.75
Low-emissivity double glazing, argonabout 1.1 to 1.6about 0.4 to 0.6
Solar control double glazingabout 1.1 to 1.6about 0.25 to 0.45

These values are indicative; ask the glass supplier for the technical data sheet of the exact product. The lower U is, the better the glazing insulates; the lower g is, the less solar heat it lets in. A g that is too low also darkens the rooms: a compromise is chosen according to the orientation.

Choosing according to orientation

  • West and south-west: low solar factor and external shading, as these are the most demanding façades in summer.
  • South: a well-sized canopy or overhang is often enough; the summer sun is very high there.
  • East: movable shading for the morning sun.
  • North: clearer glazing is possible, as direct solar gain is low.

The frame matters as much as the glass

  1. Aluminium with a thermal break: a standard aluminium profile conducts heat and can cancel out part of the glazing's benefit.
  2. PVC: a good insulator, to be chosen from a UV-resistant range.
  3. Airtightness: careful seals and installation, otherwise hot air and dust get in.

What the RTCM says

In its prescriptive approach, the Moroccan thermal building regulation sets requirements for glazed openings (U and solar factor) that depend on the climate zone and the overall glazing ratio (TGBV), i.e. the ratio between the glazed area and the area of the exterior walls. The more glazed the façade, the stricter the requirements. Marrakech is in zone 5.

What about noise? Double glazing also improves sound insulation, which is useful in town or near a road. For a real improvement, choose asymmetric glazing (two panes of different thicknesses) or acoustic laminated glass, and above all an airtight frame: noise passes first through poorly fitted seals and shutter boxes. Laminated glass also provides security against break-ins and injuries if the glass breaks.

The role of the engineering consultancy

The BET calculates the TGBV, checks compliance with the RTCM and weighs high-performance glazing against solar shading, then sizes the air conditioning accordingly. See solar shading and house orientation.

Frequently asked questions

Does double glazing protect against the heat?

Partially: clear double glazing reduces conduction but lets most solar radiation through; against the heat, you need solar control glass or external shading.

What is the solar factor of glazing?

It is the proportion of solar energy that passes through the glass, between 0 and 1; the lower it is, the less the room heats up in the sun.

Which frame should be chosen with double glazing?

An aluminium frame with a thermal break or a UV-resistant PVC frame, with airtight installation.
