Home EV charger in Morocco: power, cable and price

Charging an electric car from an ordinary socket is slow and puts strain on a circuit that is not designed for it. A wall-mounted charger (wallbox) on a dedicated circuit is the normal solution at home.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

At home, the usual solution is a 7.4 kW single-phase wallbox (32 A), or an 11 kW three-phase one if the villa has a three-phase supply. It is connected to a dedicated circuit from the board, with its own protective device and a 30 mA RCD, and a cable that is generally 6 mm², or even 10 mm² depending on the length. Moroccan price guides published in 2026 put the budget at around 3,000 to 7,000 DH for an installed 7 kW charger, but other sources give considerably higher ranges depending on the model and the work involved.

At 7.4 kW, a 50 kWh battery charges in roughly 7 to 8 hours, i.e. overnight. Higher power is only worthwhile if the vehicle accepts it and the power available at the meter allows it.

Installation rules

  • Dedicated circuit: one charging point per circuit, with nothing else connected to it. Part 7-722 of NF C 15-100 deals specifically with charging installations.
  • 30 mA RCD protection: type A, F or B depending on the charger's instructions. Many chargers include DC leakage current detection; otherwise a type B RCD is often required. Follow the manufacturer's instructions.
  • Cable size: 6 mm² is common for 32 A over a short distance; one manufacturer's standard specification requires 10 mm² for 7.4 kW. Length is the deciding factor: see voltage drop and cable length.
  • Earthing: the charger must be connected to a good-quality earth electrode.
  • Location: garage or carport, sheltered from rain and direct sun if possible; chargers have an outdoor protection rating, but Marrakech's summer heat can reduce the charging power.

Meter capacity, the real issue

7.4 kW is the equivalent of six or seven air conditioners running. If the villa already has substantial air conditioning, the charger may trip the incoming circuit breaker on a summer evening. There are two solutions: increase the power with the distributor (ONEE, Radeema, Lydec, depending on the city), which can mean a significant cost, or install a charger with dynamic load management, which reduces the charging power when the house is using a lot. The starting point is the power assessment.

Indicative budget in Morocco

Published ranges vary widely. A 2026 Moroccan guide estimates the equipment at around 2,000 to 4,000 DH for a 7 kW charger and 3,500 to 6,000 DH for an 11 kW one, plus 800 to 2,000 DH for installation and possibly 500 to 2,000 DH to upgrade the board. Other sources quote 6,000 to 25,000 DH for chargers of 7 to 22 kW, excluding any subscription upgrade. Indicative prices: ask for detailed quotes specifying the brand, cable length and RCD protection.

The role of the design office

In new construction, it is enough to provide a conduit between the board and the garage and a spare slot in the board so the charger can be added later without chasing walls. For a residential complex or apartment building, the design office studies the available power, metering and load management between several parking spaces.

Frequently asked questions

What charger power for a house?

A 7.4 kW single-phase charger (32 A) suits most cases and charges a 50 kWh battery overnight. With a three-phase supply, an 11 kW charger is possible.

What cable size for a 7.4 kW charger?

Often 6 mm² for a short distance, or 10 mm² depending on the length and the manufacturer's recommendations, on a dedicated circuit protected by a 30 mA RCD.

How much does an EV charger cost in Morocco?

A 2026 Moroccan guide estimates an installed 7 kW charger at roughly 3,000 to 7,000 DH; other sources quote up to 25,000 DH for more powerful chargers. Indicative prices.
