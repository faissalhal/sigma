Checking the electrics of an old house: 8 simple checks

An outdated electrical installation is a major cause of house fires. Eight checks tell you whether it needs making safe or completely rewiring.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

To assess the electrics of an old house, start with the consumer unit: if it has no 30 mA residual current switch or breaker, no modular circuit breakers, and the house has no earth connection, the installation needs to be redone. The following checks tell you whether making it safe is enough or a complete rewiring is required.

The 8 checks

  1. The consumer unit: pin or porcelain fuses, unlabelled circuits, burn marks or an open enclosure point to an installation from another era.
  2. 30 mA residual current protection: this is what protects people against electrocution. Press the test button: the device should trip.
  3. Earthing: check for a green-and-yellow conductor arriving at the consumer unit and an earth rod or earth loop. Two-hole sockets with no earth pin are a bad sign.
  4. Cable sizes: wires too thin for the breaker rating overheat. As a common NF C 15-100 benchmark: 1.5 mm² for lighting, 2.5 mm² for 20 A socket circuits, 6 mm² for a 32 A hob.
  5. Exposed conductors: unsheathed wires, visible connector blocks, permanent extension leads, daisy-chained multi-plugs.
  6. The bathroom: a socket or switch near the shower, a water heater with no dedicated protection, no equipotential bonding.
  7. Dedicated circuits: the oven, washing machine, water heater and air conditioners should each have their own circuit. In old houses, everything is often plugged into the same sockets.
  8. The meter and supply connection: check the contracted power with the distributor (ONEE, Radeema in Marrakech, Lydec, Redal or Amendis depending on the city) and the condition of the incoming cable.

Which technical reference?

In Morocco, low-voltage installations are governed by the Moroccan standards series NM 06.1.100 to 06.1.106 (IMANOR); NM 06.1.100 was approved in May 2025. In engineering firms' practice, NF C 15-100 remains the most widely cited technical reference; its 2024 edition replaced the 2002 document and changed some rules. An old house is never compliant with these recent texts: the realistic first objective is the safety of the occupants.

Frequently asked questions

How can you tell if a house's electrics are dangerous?

The main signs are a fuse board, no 30 mA RCD, no earth connection, exposed wires or signs of overheating. Any one of these points justifies making the installation safe.

Which electrical standard applies in Morocco?

The Moroccan standards series NM 06.1.100 to 06.1.106 governs low-voltage installations; in engineering firms' practice, NF C 15-100 remains the most widely cited technical reference.

Do you always have to redo everything in an old house?

No. If the cables are in good condition and correctly sized, a new consumer unit with 30 mA RCDs and a proper earth connection may be enough; otherwise a complete rewiring is preferable.
