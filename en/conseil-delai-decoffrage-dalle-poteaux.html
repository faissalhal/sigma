Formwork striking times for slabs, beams and columns

Striking formwork too early is one of the classic causes of excessive deflection, cracking and even collapse. Here are the times to respect depending on the element and conditions.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Vertical formwork (column sides, walls, beam sides) can generally be removed after 1 to 3 days, as it carries no load. Soffit formwork and props under slabs and beams must stay in place much longer, commonly 21 to 28 days, or be replaced by back-propping. These times are indicative: the rule is to strike only once the concrete has reached sufficient strength.

Indicative times by element

ElementUsual time (indicative)Remark
Column and wall sides1 to 3 daysWait until the edges no longer tear off when striking
Beam sides1 to 3 daysThe beam soffit and its props stay in place
Slab and beam soffitsOften 21 to 28 daysEarlier striking possible with back-propping
Cantilevers, balconiesLonger, as specified by the engineerElements very sensitive to premature striking

Concrete only reaches its design strength at 28 days, but it develops a large part of it in the first week, especially in hot weather. This is why some formwork can be removed earlier, provided props are left to carry the slab.

What makes the times vary

  • Temperature: concrete hardens faster in the heat and more slowly in cold weather (Atlas winters).
  • The type of cement and the concrete mix.
  • The span: a large slab or a long beam needs more time.
  • Construction loads: a floor that is already carrying the formwork and concrete of the floor above must remain propped.
  • Curing: concrete that has dried too quickly does not have the expected strength.

Back-propping

To reuse formwork faster, contractors use back-propping: the floor is struck and props are immediately put back to relieve it until it has reached its strength. This method is safe if it is organised bay by bay, without leaving the floor unsupported. In a multi-storey building, props over several floors often add up: a propping plan is needed.

Signs of premature striking

Visible deflection of the slab, cracks on the soffit or near the supports, chipped column edges, or honeycombing revealed too early. Excessive deflection is often irreversible. If in doubt, have the cracks examined by an engineer.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm sets the minimum striking times and conditions in the specifications, taking the season into account. For sensitive elements, it may require tests on specimens before authorising removal of the props. Proper concrete curing also determines the strength at the time of striking.

Frequently asked questions

When can a concrete slab be struck?

The soffit formwork and props generally stay in place for 21 to 28 days, unless organised back-propping is used. The exact time depends on the span, the temperature and the strength reached.

How long before striking a column?

Column sides can generally be removed after 1 to 3 days, when the concrete is hard enough for the edges not to tear off.

What is back-propping?

It means putting props back under a slab just after striking, to relieve it until it reaches its strength while allowing the formwork to be reused.
