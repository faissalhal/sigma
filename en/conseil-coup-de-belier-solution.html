Water hammer in pipes: causes and solutions that actually work

A "bang" in the walls when the washing machine stops or a mixer tap is closed: it is not just a noise, it is a pressure surge that strains the whole installation.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Water hammer is a pressure surge wave that occurs when moving water is stopped abruptly: fast closing of a mixer tap, the solenoid valve of a washing machine or dishwasher, a quarter-turn tap. It is treated by reducing the pressure (to around 3 bar), slowing the water in the pipes and fitting a water hammer arrestor close to the appliance causing the shock.

Why it bangs

Water is almost incompressible. When a column of water flowing at 2 m/s is stopped in a fraction of a second, its energy turns into a pressure spike that travels back up the pipe and makes the pipework vibrate. Tap manufacturers give examples where the surge approaches 30 bar over about ten metres of pipe. The higher the water velocity, the length of straight pipe and the mains pressure, the more violent the shock.

The most common causes

  • Excessive pressure: above 4 bar, shocks become frequent.
  • Pipes that are too small: for the same flow, the water moves faster. The general target is 1.5 to 2 m/s maximum in a dwelling.
  • Solenoid valves on washing machines, dishwashers, automatic irrigation: they close instantly.
  • Quarter-turn taps and mixer taps shut off sharply.
  • Poorly secured pipes: the pipe moves and knocks against the masonry, which amplifies the noise.
  • A poorly adjusted booster pump or one without a buffer tank, which starts and stops abruptly.

The solutions, from simplest to most complete

  1. Measure the pressure with a gauge and fit a pressure reducer set at around 3 bar if it is too high.
  2. Install a water hammer arrestor of the membrane or piston type, as close as possible to the appliance causing the shock (on the washing machine tap, for example). An arrestor fitted far from the closing point is not very effective.
  3. Redo the fixings: suitable clips roughly every metre, with insulating sleeves where pipes pass through walls.
  4. Check the booster pump: pressure vessel pre-charge, pressure switch setting, or a switch to a variable-speed pump (see booster pump and water pressure).
  5. Review the diameters on long, straight runs during a renovation (see pipe diameters).

Do not confuse it with other noises

Why you should act quickly

Every water hammer strains the fittings, flexible hoses and welds. Over time, they are the ones that give way, often inside a wall or slab. In an apartment building or a hotel, the engineering firm prevents the problem from the design stage: velocity calculations, pressure reducers on each floor, arrestors in the right places, and a choice of suitable tapware.

Frequently asked questions

How do you get rid of water hammer?

First bring the pressure down to around 3 bar with a pressure reducer, then fit a water hammer arrestor as close as possible to the appliance causing the shock and check the pipe fixings.

Where should a water hammer arrestor be placed?

As close as possible to the appliance or valve that closes abruptly, for example on the supply tap of the washing machine or dishwasher.

Is water hammer dangerous for the installation?

Over time, yes: repeated pressure surges strain fittings, flexible hoses and welds, which end up leaking, sometimes inside a wall.
