Wastewater drain slope: a minimum of 1 cm per metre of pipe run

A drain that clogs every six months almost always comes down to a badly set slope. The rules are simple, but they still need to be checked before the slab is poured.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

A drain pipe must fall by at least 1 cm per metre (a 1% slope), the minimum value cited for DTU 60.11. In practice, the aim is 2 to 3 cm per metre for greywater (washbasin, shower, sink) as well as for blackwater (WC): the water carries deposits away better and the network clogs less.

Greywater, blackwater: what are we talking about?

  • Wastewater (or greywater): washbasin, shower, bathtub, sink, washing machine, dishwasher. It contains grease, soap and hair.
  • Blackwater: WC. It carries solid matter and requires a larger diameter (100 mm).
  • Rainwater: terrace, courtyard, patio. It follows its own network, unless the public network is a combined system.

The slopes to respect

SectionMinimum slopeRecommended slope
Fixture connection (washbasin, shower, sink)1 cm/m2 to 3 cm/m
WC drain (100 mm)1 cm/m2 to 3 cm/m
Drain under the ground slab or buried1 cm/m2 cm/m or more if the sewer level allows

Below 1 cm/m, matter settles and blockages appear within a few months. A very steep slope is not ideal either over long runs: the water rushes ahead and leaves the solids behind. Where the drop is large, a vertical stack followed by a section at normal slope is preferred.

Costly mistakes

  • Back-fall: a pipe poorly bedded in the backfill sags and forms a belly where water stagnates. It needs a bed of compacted sand and support roughly every metre.
  • A poorly surveyed sewer level: if the invert of the public manhole is too high, the slope becomes impossible. Survey it before setting the ground-floor levels; otherwise, a sewage lifting station or costly remedial work will be needed.
  • 90° bends in series: use two 45° bends instead, and inspection chambers or access plugs at changes of direction.
  • A flush-floor shower tray without enough recess in the slab: the pipe's slope is then sacrificed (see walk-in shower).

Calculating the height required

The rule is simple: height = length × slope. For a shower located 6 m from the stack with a 2% slope, you need a 12 cm drop, plus the height of the trap and of the pipe itself. That is why recesses in slabs and false ceilings must be decided at the drawing stage, not at the time of installation.

Check before closing up

Before pouring a screed or backfilling, have the slopes checked with a level or laser, then carry out a flow test (and a leak-tightness test on buried sections). The engineering firm sets the invert levels on the drawings, checks that they are respected during site visits and records the points to be corrected.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum slope for a wastewater drain?

The minimum cited for DTU 60.11 is 1 cm per metre (1%); 2 to 3 cm per metre is recommended to limit deposits and blockages.

What slope for a WC drain?

As for wastewater, at least 1 cm per metre, and ideally 2 to 3 cm per metre on a 100 mm pipe, with as few bends as possible.

Is too steep a slope a problem?

Over long runs, yes: the water flows too fast and leaves solid matter behind. To make up a large drop, a vertical stack is used instead.
