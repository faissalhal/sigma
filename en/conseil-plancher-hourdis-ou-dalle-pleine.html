Hollow-block floor or solid slab: which one for your house?

The hollow-block floor is the most widespread solution for villas in Morocco, but the solid slab remains essential in certain cases. Here are the criteria to decide.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

For a villa with typical spans (up to about 5 m), the beam-and-hollow-block floor is generally the most economical and the fastest to build. The solid slab is required for irregular shapes, cantilevers, heavy loads, wet areas or when you want a smooth soffit with no downstand beams. The choice lies with the engineer, in agreement with the architect.

The hollow-block floor (hourdis)

It consists of joists (precast or cast in place), hollow concrete or clay blocks (the hourdis) and a topping slab reinforced with welded mesh. It is designated by two figures: a 16+4 floor has 16 cm blocks and a 4 cm topping, i.e. about 20 cm in total; a 20+5 is about 25 cm.

Allowable spans depend on the joists used, the loads and the reinforcement: they are read from the manufacturer's tables or calculated. The values above are orders of magnitude, to be confirmed by calculation.

The solid reinforced concrete slab

It is a reinforced concrete plate cast on formwork, supported on beams or walls. It can span in one or two directions, which makes it suitable for irregular shapes, cantilevered balconies and openings. It is heavier and uses more concrete and steel, but offers better acoustic insulation against airborne noise and great freedom for routing services.

Quick comparison

  • Cost: advantage to the hollow-block floor on typical spans, with less concrete and formwork.
  • Weight: the hollow-block floor is lighter, which reduces the loads on the columns, the foundations and the seismic force.
  • Shapes: advantage to the solid slab for corners, cantilevers and large openings.
  • Acoustics: the solid slab, being more massive, insulates better against airborne noise.
  • Fixings: drilling into a hollow block requires suitable anchors for suspended loads.

Seismic requirements

In both cases, the floor must act as a rigid diaphragm connecting the columns and walls. This requires a continuous topping slab, properly reinforced and connected to the ring beams and beams. A topping slab that is too thin or has no mesh compromises this role. On roof terraces, also think about waterproofing and insulation.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm chooses the floor type room by room, calculates spans and loads, defines the direction of the joists, the reinforcement around openings and the solid slab zones. The formwork drawings it provides prevent improvisation on site, such as a joist doubled "by eye" under a heavy partition.

Frequently asked questions

What does a 16+4 floor mean?

It is a joist floor with 16 cm high hollow blocks and a 4 cm topping slab, i.e. a total thickness of about 20 cm.

What span for a 16+4 hollow-block floor?

It is commonly used for residential spans of up to about 5 m, but the allowable span depends on the joists and loads and must be checked by calculation.

Is a solid slab stronger than a hollow-block floor?

Both are safe if they are designed. The solid slab is better suited to irregular shapes, cantilevers and heavy loads; the hollow-block floor is lighter and more economical on typical spans.
