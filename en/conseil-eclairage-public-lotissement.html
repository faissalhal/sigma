Street lighting for a subdivision: levels, poles, LED and network

How many lux in a residential street, which lamp posts, how to supply the network: the benchmarks for designing the street lighting of a subdivision.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

For a residential subdivision street, street lighting is generally designed using the P classes of the European standard EN 13201, commonly used as a reference in Moroccan specifications: they require a maintained average illuminance ranging from about 2 lux (P6) to 15 lux (P1). The level chosen depends on usage, speed and surroundings; the requirements of the municipality and the operator always take precedence.

The lighting classes

FamilyUseMain criterion
MMotorised traffic routes (boulevards, roads)Road surface luminance
CConflict areas (junctions, roundabouts)Illuminance
PResidential roads, pedestrians, cyclists, low speedHorizontal illuminance, from 2 to 15 lux depending on the sub-class

A revision of the standard has changed some criteria of the P classes; check which edition is specified in the project specifications.

Luminaires, poles and layout

  • LED: this is now the standard solution, with lower consumption and the option of dimming in the middle of the night.
  • Colour temperature: warm light limits glare and disturbance for residents and wildlife.
  • Poles: in a residential street, lamp posts are lower than on a boulevard; the height and spacing result from the photometric calculation, not from a fixed rule.
  • Layout: single-sided for a narrow road, staggered or opposite for a wider road; watch out for trees that will hide the luminaires as they grow.
  • Uniformity: a good design avoids alternating very bright and dark areas, which is more disturbing than a slightly lower average level.

An example: for a residential subdivision street, the photometric calculation compares several combinations of pole height, power and spacing, then selects the one that reaches the required level with the fewest light points. A few metres gained on the spacing, repeated across a whole subdivision, mean dozens fewer poles, foundations and luminaires, and as much maintenance saved.

The lighting electrical network

  1. A control cabinet connected to the distributor's network, with metering, protection devices and an astronomical clock or photocell.
  2. Underground cables in ducts, with red warning mesh, and draw pits where needed.
  3. Earthing of the poles and cabling sized to limit the voltage drop at the end of the line.
  4. Concrete foundations suited to the pole height and to wind loads.

Acceptance and operation

At the acceptance of the subdivision, the municipality or its operator checks the illuminance levels, the correct operation of the cabinet, the insulation tests and the as-built drawings. A network handed over without accurate drawings quickly becomes a maintenance headache. Also ask for the luminaire data sheets and warranties: they will be useful for replacements.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm carries out the photometric calculation, selects the luminaires, sizes the cables and protection devices, positions the poles consistently with the road and the planting, and prepares the application submitted to the network operator.

Frequently asked questions

How many lux for a subdivision street?

With the P classes of standard EN 13201, the maintained average illuminance ranges from about 2 lux (P6) to 15 lux (P1); the level depends on usage and on the municipality's requirements.

Should LEDs be chosen for street lighting?

Yes, it is now the usual solution: lower consumption, long service life and the option of reducing the intensity in the middle of the night.

Who maintains the street lighting of a subdivision?

The developer until acceptance; after that, the networks and equipment in principle pass to the municipality or its operator.
