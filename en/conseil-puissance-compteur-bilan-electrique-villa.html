Villa power assessment: what meter capacity to request

Adding up the power of every appliance gives a figure that is too high: they never all run at the same time. The power assessment is used to request the right connection, neither too small nor oversized.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

The power to request from the distributor is calculated through a power assessment: you list all the equipment, apply to each item a factor reflecting the fact that they do not all run at full power at the same time, then add them up. For an air-conditioned villa of 250 to 350 m² with a pool, the result is often between 15 and 25 kVA, while the raw sum of the appliances easily exceeds 30 kW. Each project remains a special case.

The four-step method

  1. Inventory: lighting, sockets, kitchen, household appliances, water heater, air conditioning, pool, pumps, gate, EV charger, outdoor areas.
  2. Power of each item: rating plate or data sheet (note: for an air conditioner, use the electrical power input, not the cooling capacity in BTU).
  3. Factors: a utilisation factor (the appliance does not always run flat out) and a simultaneity or diversity factor (they do not all run at the same time). They are always less than or equal to 1.
  4. Total and margin: conversion to kVA, margin for planned extensions, then the choice between single-phase and three-phase.

Worked example for a 300 m² villa

The factors below are illustrative values commonly used at the preliminary design stage, not regulatory values.

In this example, the power retained points towards a three-phase connection. Without the air conditioning and the induction hob, it would stay around 10 kVA. In Marrakech, air conditioning is almost always the item that makes the difference: in July, with over 40 °C outside, all the split units run at the same time, hence a high factor. To estimate air conditioner power, see air conditioning power per m².

Too low or too high: the consequences

  • Power too low: the incoming circuit breaker trips at peak times (summer evening, cooking plus air conditioning), you have to reapply to the distributor and sometimes rework the cables.
  • Power too high: higher connection and subscription charges, oversized cables and protective devices.
  • Forgetting a future project: an EV charger, pool heat pump or extension adds several kVA; it is better to include them from the start, even if they are managed by load shedding.

Ways to reduce the power required

  • A solar water heater instead of an electric water heater.
  • Proper thermal insulation (see the RTCM), which reduces the size of the air conditioners.
  • A load shedder that temporarily cuts a non-priority circuit (water heater, charger) when the power approaches the limit.
  • Inverter equipment, whose starting current is lower.

The role of the design office

The power assessment is one of the first documents in the electrical design. The design office draws it up from the plans and equipment choices, deduces the type of supply, the size of the incoming cable and the layout of the board, and provides the technical information for the application to ONEE or your city's utility (Radeema in Marrakech).

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the electrical power of a villa?

You list all the equipment, apply a utilisation and simultaneity factor to each item, then add them up. For a 300 m² air-conditioned villa, the result is often 15 to 25 kVA.

Why not simply add up all the appliances?

Because they never all run at full power at the same time: the raw sum greatly overestimates the need and would mean paying for an oversized connection.

Which item weighs the most in Marrakech?

Air conditioning: in summer, all the air conditioners run at the same time. Their electrical power input, not their BTU rating, goes into the assessment.
