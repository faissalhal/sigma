Ventilation and smoke extraction in building car parks: principles

A covered car park must be ventilated daily to remove exhaust fumes, and have smoke extraction in the event of fire to allow evacuation and firefighters' intervention.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

An underground or covered car park fulfils two ventilation functions: everyday ventilation, which keeps carbon monoxide (CO) and other pollutants below tolerable thresholds, and smoke extraction, which removes smoke in the event of fire. Both can use the same fans, provided they are designed to withstand hot smoke. Sizing requires a specialist study and must be validated as part of the project's fire safety.

The framework in Morocco

In Morocco, fire safety in buildings is governed by decree no. 2-14-499 of 15 October 2014, which approves the general building regulation laying down the safety rules against fire and panic risks. The provisions applicable to a car park depend on the type of building (residential, building open to the public, high-rise building) and the size of the car park. In practice, engineering firms also rely on foreign technical references, notably French ones, for sizing; the chosen scheme must be validated by the competent authorities or the inspection body.

Everyday ventilation

  • The aim: to remove CO and exhaust gases from vehicles driving and manoeuvring.
  • CO detection: sensors distributed throughout the car park control the ventilation according to the measured concentration, which avoids running the fans continuously.
  • Two-speed operation: low speed in normal use, high speed during traffic peaks or a CO alarm.
  • Air inlets: access ramp, grilles or air supply ducts, distributed to sweep the whole volume with no dead zones.

Smoke extraction

In the event of fire, a burning car produces a large quantity of toxic smoke. The purpose of smoke extraction is to keep areas usable for evacuation and firefighter access. According to French technical guides, the following benchmarks are commonly used (to be checked against the text applicable to the project):

Technical benchmark (French references)Order of magnitude
Extraction rate for a residential car park600 m³/h per vehicle
Car park of a building open to the public, with sprinklers600 m³/h per vehicle
Car park of a building open to the public, without sprinklers900 m³/h per vehicle
FansCertified resistance to hot smoke (for example class F400-120 or F200-120 depending on the case)
  • Compartmentation: large car parks are divided into compartments or smoke zones, each with its own smoke extraction.
  • The power supply to the fans must be maintained during the fire: fire-resistant cables, back-up supply depending on the case.
  • Control: automatic via fire detection and manual from a station accessible to the emergency services.
  • Ducts and dampers passing through other rooms must retain their fire resistance.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Placing air discharges near the windows or fresh air intakes of the building.
  • Forgetting the openings in the structure for ducts and fans: creating them after pouring is expensive.
  • Neglecting fan noise for the apartments above.
  • Never testing the installation: acceptance tests and regular maintenance are essential.

The role of the engineering firm

The MEP and fire safety engineering firm defines the principle (natural or mechanical, see natural or mechanical smoke extraction), calculates the flow rates, chooses the fans, coordinates the ducts with the structure and electrical work, and compiles the fire safety file. For a building open to the public, see also our article on fire safety of public buildings in Morocco.

Frequently asked questions

What smoke extraction rate for a car park?

French references commonly use 600 m³/h per vehicle for a residential car park, and 600 or 900 m³/h per vehicle for the car park of a building open to the public depending on whether sprinklers are present; the rate must be validated against the text applicable to the project.

Must an underground car park have CO detection?

CO detection is the usual solution for controlling the ventilation of an underground car park: it triggers high speed when the concentration rises and avoids ventilating continuously.

Which text governs fire safety in buildings in Morocco?

Decree no. 2-14-499 of 15 October 2014, which approves the general building regulation laying down the safety rules against fire and panic risks.
