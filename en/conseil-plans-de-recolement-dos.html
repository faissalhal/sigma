As-built drawings and DOE: the file to demand at the end of works

In five years' time, when a leak appears under the tiles or a circuit breaker trips, this file will save you days of searching and a lot of money.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

As-built drawings (plans de récolement) show what was actually built, not what was planned. They are part of the DOE (dossier des ouvrages exécutés, as-built works file), the set of documents describing the delivered building: drawings, diagrams, technical data sheets, manuals and test reports. You must demand them from the contractor before final acceptance.

Why this file is essential

  • Maintenance and repairs: locating a concealed pipe, a buried inspection chamber or a junction box without breaking things at random.
  • Future works: drilling through a slab, opening a bay or adding a storey without hitting a beam or a network.
  • Resale: a buyer and their surveyor appreciate a documented building.
  • Disputes and insurance: proving what was carried out.

Typical content of a villa DOE

Trade packageExpected documents
Structural workAs-built formwork and reinforcement drawings, concrete test results if carried out
PlumbingRoutes of supply and drainage pipes, inspection chambers, septic tank or sewer connection, pressure tests
ElectricalSingle-line diagrams of the panels, circuit labelling, earth electrode measurement
Air conditioning, ventilationLocation of units, refrigerant and condensate pipe routes, appliance data sheets
External works and utilitiesDimensioned buried networks (depth, distances from reference points), irrigation, lighting
EquipmentManuals, manufacturers' warranties, maintenance contracts

How to obtain it

  1. Provide for it in the contract and in the CPS, with its format (paper and digital files).
  2. Photograph the networks before they are closed up: trenches, conduits in slabs, chases before rendering, with a tape measure visible. This is the best insurance if the drawings are approximate.
  3. Tie the final payment or the release of the retention money to the delivery of the complete file, checked and accepted in writing.
  4. Demand usable files (DWG or IFC in addition to PDF) so that the drawings can be modified during future works.
  5. Have the file checked by the BET before accepting it.

Administrative as-built inspection: not to be confused

The word “récolement” also refers to the visit by the municipal commission before the occupancy permit is issued. This visit compares the building with the building permit; it does not replace the technical DOE, which is for the owner.

How to archive it. Keep a paper copy in the house, near the electrical panel, and a digital copy (PDFs, dated photos) in a backed-up location. Hand it over to the buyer if you sell, and update it with every alteration: new bathroom, additional air conditioner, extension. For an apartment building or a hotel, appoint someone responsible for the file, because operators change and knowledge is quickly lost.

The role of the engineering consultancy

The BET that designed the networks is best placed to check the contractor's as-built drawings, complete the diagrams and put together a consistent file. With a BIM model, the DOE becomes a searchable digital model.

Frequently asked questions

What is an as-built drawing?

It is the drawing of the building as it was actually constructed, with the exact position of the networks and built elements, as opposed to the design drawings.

Who must provide the DOE?

Each contractor provides the documents for its trade package; the contract must provide for this, and its delivery can be a condition for the final payment or the release of the retention money.

How can you protect yourself if the contractor does not provide reliable drawings?

Systematically photograph the networks before they are closed up (trenches, slabs, chases) with a tape measure and reference points visible.
