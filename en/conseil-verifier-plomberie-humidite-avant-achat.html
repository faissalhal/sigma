Checking plumbing and damp before buying: a practical guide

Water is the leading cause of defects in a house. A few simple tests during the viewing will stop you buying a hidden leak or a wall that never dries.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Before buying, check the plumbing with three tests: read the water meter at the start and end of the viewing without turning anything on, open several taps at the same time to judge the pressure, and watch how sinks, showers and toilets drain. For damp, inspect the base of the walls, the top-floor ceilings, below-ground walls and bathrooms. These checks take twenty minutes.

Plumbing: tests and warning signs

  • Meter turning with taps closed: a leak in the system, sometimes buried or concealed in walls. See our article on how to detect a hidden water leak.
  • Pipe material: the galvanised steel found in old houses scales up and rusts from the inside, reducing flow; PEX, multilayer, PPR and copper are current materials.
  • Low pressure upstairs: scaled-up pipework, insufficient diameter or no booster pump. In Marrakech, hard water speeds up scaling.
  • Water heater: age, signs of rust, safety valve group present, protected electrical supply.
  • Drains: slow draining, gurgling, sewage odours. These indicate insufficient slope, a faulty trap or unventilated soil stacks.

Drainage

Ask whether the house is connected to the public sewer (Radeema in Marrakech) or has a septic tank. For a tank, have the inspection cover opened, ask when it was last emptied and check the distance from any well. Blocked inspection chambers or odours in the garden mean the pipes will need repairing.

Damp: identifying the cause

WhereWhat you seeLikely cause
Bottom of ground-floor wallsEfflorescence, plaster crumbling up to about 1 mRising damp
Top-floor ceilingStains, blistered paintFailed roof terrace waterproofing
Below-ground walls, basementSeepage, water marksNo drainage or tanking
Bathroom, adjacent wallsBlack grout, hollow-sounding tilesWaste pipe leak or failed waterproofing under the tiles
Wall corners, behind furnitureMould, odourCondensation, inadequate ventilation

The cost of repair depends mainly on the cause: adding ventilation costs little, redoing roof terrace waterproofing or treating rising damp much more. See our article on flat roof waterproofing.

Common traps

  • A room repainted just before the sale or a new suspended ceiling can hide marks.
  • A viewing in the height of summer in Marrakech will not show water ingress that only appears with the winter rains.
  • A dehumidifier or wide-open windows during the viewing are clues worth questioning.

Assessment by an engineering firm

A technician equipped with a moisture meter and, if needed, a thermal camera locates the damp areas and works out the cause. They also check drainage slopes and the condition of the pipework. Frequently asked questions

How can you detect a leak during a viewing?

Read the water meter at the start of the viewing and at the end, without using any water in between: if it has moved, there is a leak in the system.

How do you tell rising damp from water ingress?

Rising damp affects the bottom of the walls to a fairly even height, often with efflorescence; water ingress tends to appear on ceilings, around windows and doors, or after rain.

Should galvanised steel pipes be replaced?

In an old house, often yes: they scale up and rust from the inside, which reduces pressure and eventually causes leaks.
