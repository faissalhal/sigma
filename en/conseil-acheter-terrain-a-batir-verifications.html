Buying building land in Morocco: the checks to carry out

Land advertised as buildable is not always so, or not for the project you have in mind. Here are the legal, planning and technical checks to carry out before signing.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Before buying building land, carry out three checks: legal (land title, owner, mortgages), planning (the urban planning information note and the development plan, which say what you can build) and technical (soil, slope, access and utilities). The urban planning information note is the document most often forgotten and the most decisive.

The legal side

  • Land title: obtain a recent certificate of ownership from the land registry (ANCFCC). It shows the registered owner, the area, mortgages, objections and easements.
  • Boundaries: compare the title plan with the actual plot; if in doubt, bring in a land surveyor.
  • Untitled land (melk): buying is possible but riskier; get a notary to assist you.
  • Agricultural status: land outside the urban perimeter may be agricultural. Building and certain buyers are then subject to special conditions, including the certificate of non-agricultural status; check with the notary.

The planning side

Request a recent urban planning information note from the urban agency or the municipality. It states the development plan zoning, the permitted height and number of storeys, setbacks, the plot ratio, easements and any land reserved for planned roads or public facilities. As a development plan can be revised, an old note may be out of date.

InformationWhy it matters
Zoning (villa, apartment building, agricultural…)Determines the type of construction allowed
Height, number of storeysLimits the buildable floor area
Setbacks from the road and boundariesReduces the actual building footprint
Land reserved for a planned roadMay take away part of the plot
Easements (power lines, wadi, heritage)May prohibit or restrict construction

The technical side

  • The soil: swelling clay, backfill, rock near the surface or a shallow water table change the type of foundation and the budget. A soil survey before buying, or at least a geotechnical opinion, avoids nasty surprises.
  • The slope: sloping land requires earthworks and sometimes costly retaining walls.
  • Access: a road wide enough for construction machinery and, later, for the civil protection services.
  • Utilities: distance to the water, electricity and sewer networks. A distant connection or a network extension can be expensive; without a sewer, you will need a septic tank.
  • Rainwater: a plot at a low point or near a wadi must be examined for flood risk.

Getting the project costed before buying

The right reflex is to have an architect produce a sketch and an engineering firm make a first estimate of the foundations and utilities before signing. This small investment tells you whether the project fits the budget, and lets you negotiate the price if the land requires special foundations or a long utility connection. Frequently asked questions

How can you tell if land is buildable in Morocco?

Request a recent urban planning information note from the urban agency or the municipality: it states the zoning and the building rules that apply to the plot.

Do you need a soil survey before buying land?

It is not compulsory before buying, but a geotechnical opinion avoids buying land whose foundations will cost much more than expected (clay, backfill, water table).

What is the VNA?

The certificate of non-agricultural status (VNA) confirms that land is not for agricultural use; it applies in particular to land located outside urban areas. The notary will tell you when it is required.
