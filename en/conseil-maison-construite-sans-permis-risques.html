House built without a permit or non-compliant: what are the risks?

An added storey, an undeclared extension, a house without an occupancy permit: these situations are common. They expose the buyer to legal, financial and sometimes technical risks.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Buying a house built without a permit, or different from the approved plans, means taking on an urban planning offence. The risks are of three kinds: legal (offence report, bringing into compliance or demolition of the illegal parts), financial (harder to get a mortgage, complicated resale) and technical (construction carried out without an architect or engineering firm). Before signing, you must measure precisely the gap with the authorisations.

The most common situations

  • No building permit at all: common on the outskirts of towns or in rural areas.
  • Permit obtained but construction different: extra storey, encroachment on the setback, covered terrace, undeclared basement.
  • No occupancy permit: the construction is finished but compliance has never been certified by the municipality.

The legal framework

Law 12-90 on urban planning makes construction subject to authorisation and provides, after completion, for the occupancy permit (permis d'habiter) or certificate of compliance. Law 66-12 on the control and punishment of urban planning and construction offences strengthened checks and penalties: offence report, stop-work order, fines and, depending on the case, demolition. It also introduced a regularisation permit for certain non-regulatory constructions; a 2023 decree opened a new two-year window for submitting applications. As deadlines may have expired, ask the municipality or the urban agency about the current options.

The consequences for the buyer

RiskIn practice
Administrative penaltyBringing into compliance or demolition of unauthorised parts, at the owner's expense
MortgageBanks often ask for the occupancy permit or the authorisations
ResaleMuch harder to sell, discount on price, wary buyers
Future workNew authorisation (additional storey, extension) difficult to obtain
SafetyStructure not calculated, risk in the event of an earthquake or overloading

The technical risk, often underestimated

A construction without a permit is often a construction without reinforced concrete drawings or inspection. An added storey can overload foundations and columns designed for a single level. Before any regularisation, an engineer must check the stability, particularly with regard to the seismic code, and recommend any reinforcements.

What to do before buying?

  1. Require the seller to provide the building permit, the approved plans and the occupancy permit.
  2. Compare these plans with the actual building, level by level.
  3. Ask the municipality whether an offence report exists and whether regularisation is possible.
  4. Have a structural assessment carried out on the unauthorised parts.
  5. Factor the cost and time of regularisation into the negotiation, or walk away.
  6. Have the exact administrative status of the property stated in the preliminary agreement and, if possible, a condition precedent linked to regularisation.

Frequently asked questions

Can you buy a house without an occupancy permit in Morocco?

It is legally possible, but risky: compliance has not been certified, a mortgage may be refused and resale will be more difficult. Check why there is no occupancy permit before signing.

Can a non-compliant construction be regularised?

Law 66-12 provided for a regularisation permit for certain non-regulatory constructions, with application deadlines set by decree; ask the municipality about the current options.

What technical risks does a storey added without a permit present?

The original foundations and columns may not have been designed for this load. A structural engineer must check the stability before any purchase or regularisation.
