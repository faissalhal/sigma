Decennial guarantee in Morocco: Article 769 of the DOC and RCD insurance

In Morocco, decennial liability has existed since the Dahir of Obligations and Contracts, but its scope is narrower than in France. What it covers, who it targets and how to protect yourself.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Yes, a decennial guarantee exists in Morocco. Article 769 of the Dahir of Obligations and Contracts (DOC) makes the architect or engineer and the contractor who dealt directly with the client liable for ten years after completion of the works if the building collapses in whole or in part, or threatens to collapse, due to a construction defect, the materials or the soil.

What Article 769 covers

  • Total or partial collapse of the building.
  • Obvious threat of collapse: serious structural defects (progressive through-cracks, foundation subsidence).
  • The causes: defective materials, defective soil, faulty construction or design.

This wording is more restrictive than French law, which also covers defects that make the building unfit for its purpose. A water infiltration or an equipment defect therefore falls rather under ordinary contractual liability.

Who is liable?

PartyCovered by Article 769?
Architect, engineer or BET contracted directly by the clientYes
Contractor who dealt directly with the clientYes
Subcontractor, supplierNo, under this article

This liability is a matter of public policy: a contract clause that would remove or reduce it has no effect. The starting point (completion or handover) is debated in legal doctrine; a written and dated handover avoids any ambiguity.

RCD insurance, mandatory since late 2024

Liability is useless if the liable party is insolvent. Law No. 59-13 created two mandatory insurance policies: contractors' all risks (TRC), paid by the client during the works, and decennial civil liability (RCD), paid by architects, engineers and contractors. These obligations came into force on 30 December 2024 according to the insurance supervisory authority and the insurers' federation. Their exact scope (types of works, permits issued after that date, possible exemptions) must be confirmed with an insurer or a lawyer.

How to protect yourself in practice

  1. Sign direct, written contracts with the architect, the BET and the general contractor.
  2. Ask for TRC and RCD insurance certificates before work starts.
  3. Have a soil investigation carried out: defective soil is a classic cause of collapse.
  4. Keep the drawings, calculation notes, site reports and handover reports.
  5. React quickly when a defect appears: have it officially recorded, then consult a lawyer, as the time limits for taking action can be short.

What about other defects? Defects that do not amount to collapse (infiltrations, non-structural cracks, faulty equipment, finishes) are dealt with under ordinary contractual liability or under the guarantees provided for in the contract: guarantee period between provisional and final acceptance, retention money, manufacturers' warranties. Hence the value of precise snag items at handover and of a contract that provides for a guarantee period and sufficient retention.

The role of the engineering consultancy

The BET is liable for the structural soundness of the building: calculation notes compliant with RPS 2011, construction drawings and reinforcement inspection reduce the risk at source. These documents are also the ones examined by the insurer and the inspection body.

Frequently asked questions

How long does the decennial guarantee last in Morocco?

Ten years after completion of the works, under Article 769 of the DOC, for the architect or engineer and the contractor who dealt directly with the client.

Does the Moroccan decennial guarantee cover water infiltration?

In principle no: Article 769 targets total or partial collapse or the threat of collapse. Infiltrations fall rather under ordinary contractual liability.

Is decennial insurance mandatory?

Yes, Law No. 59-13 made TRC and RCD mandatory, in force since 30 December 2024; the precise scope should be confirmed with the insurer.
