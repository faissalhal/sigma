Horizontal and vertical ring beams: role and rules in Morocco

Ring beams and ties encircle masonry walls and connect them to the floors. In a seismic region like Marrakech, they make the difference between a wall that cracks and a wall that collapses.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

A ring beam or tie is a reinforced concrete element built into the masonry: horizontal at each floor level and at the top of walls, vertical at corners, wall intersections and the edges of large openings. Together, they form a cage that stops the walls from breaking apart under an earthquake or settlement.

Why ring beams and ties are essential

Brick or block masonry resists compression well, but tension and shear very poorly. During an earthquake, unconfined walls separate at the corners and topple. The 2023 Al Haouz earthquake showed the gap between confined buildings and traditional masonry without ties. Ring beams and ties also transmit floor forces to the walls and limit cracking due to shrinkage or thermal movement.

The RPS rules for confined masonry

The Moroccan seismic code devotes a chapter to confined masonry buildings. The RPS 2000 text notably provides for the following, to be checked in the 2011 version applied to your project:

ElementRPS 2000 provision
End vertical tiesAt least 4 T10 bars, with stirrups spaced at 10 cm
Horizontal ring beamsMinimum steel area of 3 cm²
Concrete sectionMinimum depth of 15 cm for vertical ties and horizontal ring beams
Spacing5 m maximum between two vertical ties

Some presentation documents on RPS 2011 quote other minimum values; if in doubt, the official text and the engineering firm's design calculations prevail.

Where to place ring beams and ties

  • Horizontal: at each floor level, at the top of walls (including parapets) and, on tall walls, at mid-height if the calculation requires it.
  • Vertical: at all corners, at wall junctions, on both sides of large openings, and at regular intervals along long walls.
  • Frames: large openings are bordered by reinforced concrete stiffeners.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Horizontal ring beams interrupted at corners, with no return or lapping of the bars.
  • Vertical ties not anchored into the tie beams at the bottom or into the top ring beam.
  • Stirrups spaced too far apart or closed without hooks, bars placed directly against the brick with no cover.
  • Masonry built with no connection to the ties cast afterwards.

Ring beams and column-beam frames

In a villa with a reinforced concrete frame (columns, beams, floors), infill walls are not load-bearing, but they must be held so that they do not collapse: the tying or stiffening rules also apply to large panels. The engineering firm specifies on its drawings which structural system is adopted and where to place the stiffeners. See also our article on RPS 2011. During site visits, it checks the continuity of the ring beams at corners and their anchorage into the tie beams and floors.

Frequently asked questions

What reinforcement for a vertical tie?

RPS 2000 provides for at least 4 T10 bars with stirrups spaced at 10 cm for end vertical ties in confined masonry; the engineer may require more.

How many metres apart should vertical ties be?

RPS 2000 limits the distance between two vertical ties to 5 m, in addition to those placed at corners and wall intersections.

Is a horizontal ring beam mandatory at every storey?

Yes, in confined masonry a horizontal ring beam encircles the walls at each floor level and at the top of the wall, to connect walls and floors.
