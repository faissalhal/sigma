30 mA RCD type AC, A or F: which one for which circuit?

Not all 30 mA residual current switches detect the same leaks. The right type depends on the appliances connected, and the question matters in a Moroccan villa heavily equipped with air conditioning.

Published 16 November 2026 · 3 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

Type AC detects standard alternating current leaks: it is suitable for lighting circuits, sockets and simple heaters. Type A also detects leaks with a DC component produced by electronics: NF C 15-100 requires it for the hob, the washing machine and the electric vehicle charging point. Type F adds detection of currents from single-phase frequency converters: it is recommended for inverter air conditioners, heat pumps and variable-speed pumps.

The selection table

TypeWhat it detectsCircuits concerned
ACSinusoidal alternating leaksLighting, sockets, water heater, simple heaters
AAC + pulsating DC leaksHob, washing machine, charging point, and any circuit where you want more margin
FA + multi-frequency currents (single-phase drives)Inverter air conditioner and heat pump, variable-speed pool pump, freezer (fewer nuisance trips)
BF + smooth DC leaksThree-phase equipment with drive or rectifier, some charging points

Simple rule: a type A can always replace a type AC, and a type F a type A, but never the reverse where the higher type is required. Type A costs on average around 20% more than type AC, depending on the manufacturer.

What the standard says

  • In the 2002 edition of NF C 15-100 as amended, still widely applied: all circuits are protected by 30 mA residual current devices, and at least one type A residual current switch protects the hob and the washing machine.
  • In the 2024 edition, in force in France since September 2025, manufacturers state that type F is required at the head of circuits supplying equipment with single-phase variable-speed drives. The details depend on the text applied: to be confirmed by the inspection body for your project.
  • In Morocco, the NM 06.1.100 to 06.1.106 series of standards governs low-voltage installations; in specifications (CPS), NF C 15-100 remains the most cited reference.

The case of air-conditioned villas in Marrakech

A villa may have six to ten inverter air conditioning units, a variable-speed pool pump and a booster pump. All these machines produce leakage currents that type AC detects poorly: either it does not trip when it should, or it trips for no apparent reason. Grouping this equipment under one or more type F residual current devices, while limiting the number of appliances per device, reduces nuisance tripping in the middle of summer. The freezer also benefits from a separate residual current device (type F or "high immunity") to avoid losing its contents after an unnoticed power cut.

Distributing the residual current devices properly

The choice of type goes hand in hand with the distribution of circuits: how many residual current devices, and which circuits under each. See the number of residual current switches according to floor area and the difference between a circuit breaker and a residual current device. In a project overseen by an engineering firm, this choice appears on the single-line diagram, which prevents substitution with cheaper equipment on site.

Frequently asked questions

Which RCD for a washing machine?

The washing machine must be placed downstream of a 30 mA type A residual current switch, like the hob, according to NF C 15-100.

Which RCD for inverter air conditioning?

Manufacturers recommend a 30 mA type F residual current device for single-phase inverter air conditioners and heat pumps, as it detects the currents produced by their variable-speed drive.

Can a type AC RCD be replaced with a type A?

Yes, a type A performs all the functions of type AC and more; the reverse is prohibited where type A is required.
