Curing concrete in hot weather: best practice in Marrakech

When it is over 40 °C in Marrakech, poorly protected concrete can lose a significant part of its strength and become covered in cracks within a few hours.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

In hot weather, concrete must be poured during the cool hours and kept moist for several days: regular watering, wet sheets or hessian, or a sprayed curing compound. Cement manufacturers' guides recommend special measures as soon as the temperature exceeds 25 °C. In Marrakech, where summer temperatures often exceed 40 °C, these precautions are not optional.

Why heat damages concrete

Concrete hardens through a chemical reaction between cement and water, called hydration. If the water evaporates too quickly, this reaction stops at the surface: the concrete becomes porous, weaker and less protective for the reinforcement. Heat also speeds up setting, which makes placing and vibration harder. The result: plastic shrinkage cracks on the surface of slabs, crazing, loss of strength. According to technical sources, concrete cured in a dry environment can lose a significant part of its strength compared with concrete kept moist.

Before and during pouring

  • Pour early in the morning (or late in the day), never during the hottest hours.
  • Dampen the formwork and the substrate (blinding concrete, hollow blocks) without leaving standing water.
  • Protect aggregates and water from the sun for site-mixed concrete; for ready-mixed concrete, ask for a suitable mix (set retarder).
  • Never add water to the concrete to make up for its stiffness: this is the leading cause of weak concrete.
  • Pour quickly, with a pump if possible, and vibrate properly.
  • Postpone the pour if conditions (hot wind, extreme heat) do not allow proper work.

After pouring: curing

MethodHow to apply it
WateringMisting or fine watering several times a day, without letting it dry between passes
Sheets, wet hessianLaid as soon as the surface allows, kept moist; light-coloured mats facing the sun
Curing compoundSprayed just after finishing, it forms a film that limits evaporation
Leaving formwork in placeFormed faces dry more slowly as long as the formwork remains in place

The curing period depends on the cement, the temperature and the element; several days are a minimum in hot weather, and the specifications or the engineering firm may set a precise duration.

The most sensitive elements

Slabs and roof terraces, highly exposed to sun and wind, are the most vulnerable: their large surface area encourages evaporation. Swimming pool walls and tanks, which must remain watertight, deserve particularly careful curing. Neglected curing also reduces the effectiveness of the reinforcement cover.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm includes hot-weather concreting requirements in the specifications and checks compliance during site visits. It can also have test specimens taken to check the actual strength of the concrete before approving formwork removal.

Frequently asked questions

Above what temperature are precautions needed when pouring concrete?

Cement manufacturers' guides recommend special measures as soon as the temperature exceeds 25 °C: pouring during the cool hours, protection and enhanced curing.

How long should a concrete slab be watered in summer?

Several days at least in hot weather, keeping the surface constantly moist; the exact duration may be set by the specifications or the engineering firm.

Can water be added to concrete when it is hot?

No: added water reduces strength and increases porosity. Use a retarding admixture instead, or pour during cooler hours.
