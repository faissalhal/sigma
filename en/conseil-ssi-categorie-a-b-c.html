Fire safety system (SSI) category A to E: understanding the system

The SSI is the brain of a building's fire safety. Its category, from A to E, sets what it detects, what it triggers and how it raises the alarm.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

An SSI (fire safety system) combines fire detection and the building's safety actions: alarm, closing of fire doors, smoke control, shutdown of ventilation, unlocking of exits. It is classified into five categories, from A (the most comprehensive, with automatic detection) to E (the simplest). This classification comes from French standards (NF S 61-931 and following), which serve as a technical reference in Moroccan projects; the required category results from the type of establishment, its occupancy and the fire safety regulation (Decree 2-14-499).

The five categories

CategoryDetectionAssociated alarm equipment (usual)Examples
AAutomatic (detectors) and manualType 1, with safety control unit (CMSI)Large hotels, clinics, department stores, high-rise buildings
BManual (call points)Type 2a, with CMSIMedium-sized ERP with smoke control or interlocked doors
CManualType 2b, simplified controlsMedium-sized establishments
DManualType 3, manual safety actionsSmall establishments
EManual or noneType 4 (simple alarm, sometimes battery-powered)Small premises, small shops

This correspondence is the most common, but not all sources present it in the same way, and the regulation may require a particular combination for a given type of establishment.

The components of a category A SSI

  • The SDI (fire detection system): automatic detectors, manual call points and ECS (control and indicating equipment).
  • The CMSI (centralised fire safety control unit): it controls the actuated safety devices.
  • The DAS (actuated safety devices): self-closing fire doors, fire dampers, smoke control dampers and fans, unlocking of exits.
  • Audible and visual alarm devices.

How to choose the right category

  1. Classify the establishment (type of activity, occupancy) according to the Moroccan regulation.
  2. List the safety functions needed: smoke control, compartmentation, ventilation shutdown, lifts.
  3. Deduce the minimum category, then check whether a higher category is justified (sleeping areas, old building, value of assets, insurer's requirement).

A hotel or riad with bedrooms almost always justifies automatic detection: see our article on fire detection for hotels and riads.

Installation and maintenance

Installation follows the design and installation rules of the NF S 61-9xx series of standards (in particular NF S 61-970 for detection). It ends with acceptance tests, function by function. After that, a maintenance contract with periodic inspections, a safety logbook and staff trained to read the control panel are essential: a poorly maintained SSI generates false alarms and ends up being disabled.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm determines the SSI category, draws up the functional analysis (which zone triggers which device), sizes the equipment, writes the specifications and attends the acceptance tests, in coordination with the smoke control study.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a category A and B SSI?

Category A includes automatic detection by detectors in addition to manual call points; category B relies on manual call points, with a safety control unit.

What is a CMSI?

The centralised fire safety control unit, which automatically or manually controls the safety devices: fire doors, smoke control, ventilation shutdown, unlocking of exits.

Who determines a building's SSI category?

It results from the classification of the establishment under the fire safety regulation; the engineering firm proposes it and has it validated during the project review.
