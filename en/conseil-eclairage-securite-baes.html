Emergency lighting and BAES units: layout and maintenance rules

When the power goes out in the middle of a fire, emergency units are the only thing guiding occupants to the exit. Here is how to position them.

Published 16 November 2026 · 5 min read · SIGMA ICC, engineering consultancy

A BAES (self-contained emergency lighting unit) switches on automatically when normal lighting fails. For evacuation, the usual benchmark is a 45-lumen unit with 1 hour of autonomy, placed at each exit, at each change of direction and at least every 15 m in corridors. These rules come from French standards (NF C 71-800 for the units) and ERP regulations; in Morocco, the project requirements stem from the fire safety regulation (Decree 2-14-499).

The three functions

FunctionBenchmarkWhere
Evacuation (way-marking)45 lm unit, 1 h autonomy, green pictogramExits, changes of direction, obstacles, staircases, every 15 m maximum
Ambient (anti-panic)At least 5 lumens per m² of room area for 1 hLarge rooms open to the public: restaurant, meeting room, shop
Residential (BAEH)Low-output unit (8 lm) with long autonomy (5 h)Sleeping areas and their corridors: hotels, riads, clinics

Positioning the units properly

  • A unit above or immediately next to each exit door and each emergency exit.
  • Units at each change of direction and in front of each obstacle or change of level (steps).
  • A unit visible from every point of the corridor: the next one must always be in sight.
  • Units supplied by the same circuit as the room's normal lighting, so that they come on when that lighting fails.
  • In a riad, recessed or discreet models exist; discretion must never take precedence over visibility.

In staircases, it is customary to mark each landing; in large rooms, ambient units are distributed to light the floor evenly and avoid shadowy areas. Pictograms follow a standardised design (figure running towards a door, direction arrow) and must remain legible from a distance, without being hidden by a sign, a curtain or decoration.

Choosing self-testing units

SATI units (integrated automatic test system) test their own lamp and battery and signal a fault with an indicator light. They make maintenance much easier and are now the standard choice. In large buildings, addressable systems report faults to a central station.

Maintenance

  1. Regularly check the units' indicator lights.
  2. Have the actual autonomy checked every year by a qualified company and record the result in the safety logbook.
  3. Replace batteries at the end of their life: the heat of Marrakech summers speeds up their ageing, particularly in roof spaces and non-air-conditioned rooms.

The role of the engineering firm

The engineering firm draws up the emergency lighting plan within the electrical package, consistent with the escape routes and the fire safety system, chooses the type of units and checks the supply circuits.

Frequently asked questions

What distance between two emergency units?

A maximum of 15 m in corridors, plus a unit at each exit, at each change of direction and in front of each obstacle.

What autonomy for a BAES?

An evacuation unit provides 45 lumens for 1 hour; residential units (BAEH) for sleeping areas offer a lower output for about 5 hours.

What is a SATI unit?

A unit with an integrated automatic test system, which checks its own lamp and battery and signals any fault with an indicator light.
